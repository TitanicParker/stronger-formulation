# Meet the Team

## Relevant Expertise, Professional Connections and the Galway Neurology Environment, 2013–2021

## Purpose

This document maps the real-world clinical actors and expertise surrounding the case.

It is not a guilt map.

It does not assume that every person named here saw the patient, knew every fact, contributed to every decision, or personally applied every area of expertise represented in their publications.

Its purpose is narrower and more useful:

1. identify the actual clinicians and professional network around the case;
2. identify the expertise they publicly demonstrated during the relevant period;
3. distinguish **departmental capability** from **patient-specific knowledge**;
4. identify where specialist expertise demonstrably entered this patient's case;
5. provide comparators for the sophistication of the final discharge synthesis;
6. ask what clinical work supports the residual departmental conclusion that the relationship between the neurological disorder and the painful feet was **“Not Clear.”**

The central distinction is:

> **Capability is not proof of application. But once sophisticated capability demonstrably enters the patient's own formulation, the question becomes what clinical work supported the conclusions that formulation reached — and the questions it left unresolved.**

---

# Anchor Exhibit 1

## JAMA Neurology 2013 — *Parkin Disease: A Clinicopathologic Entity?*

**Doherty KM, Silveira-Moriyama L, Parkkinen L, et al.**
*Parkin Disease: A Clinicopathologic Entity?*
**JAMA Neurology. 2013;70(5):571–579.**
DOI: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2013.172.

Two people from the later patient-specific network appear as authors:

* **Daniel G. Healy**
* **Timothy J. Counihan**

Healy was affiliated with Beaumont Hospital; Counihan with the School of Medicine, National University of Ireland Galway.

The paper addressed **parkin/PARK2 disease**, described as the commonest genetic cause of autosomal-recessive juvenile- and young-onset parkinsonism. Its cases had a mean onset age of 34 years. Presenting signs included tremor, often with **dystonia**, and later features included freezing of gait and postural deformity. The authors characterised Parkin disease as an early-onset, slowly progressive, levodopa-responsive parkinsonian condition.

The authorship contributions are also relevant. Healy participated in study design and analysis/interpretation; Counihan contributed acquisition of data.

## Why this matters

This paper predates the patient's 2017 admission by four years.

It shows that **Healy and Counihan were already professionally connected through Parkin/PARK2 research before this patient entered Galway Neurology**.

That is materially different from discovering, after the event, that both doctors happened to know something about genetics.

The professional connection already existed in the exact disease family that became central to the patient's discharge synthesis.

The proper inference is limited but important:

> **PARK2/parkin disease was not remote expertise imported into the case from an unrelated external source. It already sat within a professional network connecting the Galway department to Healy.**

What this does **not** prove is when either clinician first considered PARK2 in this patient or precisely what either knew before the Protest.

# Anchor Exhibit 2

## Neurology 2017 — *Clinical Reasoning: A demure teenager and her dystonic foot*

**Cullinane PW, Browne P, Hennessy MJ, Counihan TJ.**
*Clinical Reasoning: A demure teenager and her dystonic foot.*
**Neurology. 2017;89(7):e71–e75.**
DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000004231.

The paper was published online on **14 August 2017**, shortly after the patient's July 2017 admission. Its authors included:

* Patrick W. Cullinane
* **Patrick Browne**
* Michael J. Hennessy
* **Timothy J. Counihan**

The publication identifies the authors with the **Department of Neurology, University Hospital Galway / National University of Ireland Galway**.

The case concerned a **13-year-old girl with a four-year history of abnormal gait**. The presenting gait abnormality involved running awkwardly on the balls of her feet and deterioration with sustained exercise.

Its indexed subject matter includes:

* dystonia;
* neurological gait disorder;
* parkinsonian disorders;
* genetics;
* parkin protein.

The clinical reasoning moved from an apparent foot-drop-type presentation to recognition of **dystonic foot phenomenology**, and ultimately to parkin-related juvenile parkinsonism.

## Why this matters

This publication is unusually close to the clinical concepts at issue here.

The relevant cluster is:

> **young patient → abnormal gait → apparently peripheral/foot presentation → dystonic reinterpretation → parkinsonian signs → parkin genetics**

And the relevant Galway actors are **Browne and Counihan**.

This is therefore a much stronger comparator than saying merely that Galway was “a sophisticated neurology department.”

It demonstrates that, contemporaneously, clinicians within that department were explicitly reasoning through the relationship between:

* abnormal foot posture;
* gait;
* dystonia;
* young-onset parkinsonism;
* and parkin disease.

The article appeared only weeks after the patient's admission.

That timing must be handled carefully.

It does **not** prove that the published article itself informed the bedside examination in July.

It does establish that the reasoning reflected in the paper belonged to the contemporary expertise environment of the Galway Neurology Department, rather than representing knowledge developed many years later.

The forensic comparison is therefore:

> **How did a department capable of publishing the dystonic-foot/Parkin relationship in a young patient contemporaneously examine and resolve the painful, dystonic-looking lower-limb/foot presentation physically available in this patient's admission?**

# Dr Aaron Brennan

## General Practitioner — the input comparator

Galway East Medical Practice records that Aaron Brennan graduated from medicine at NUI Galway in 1998 and worked for several years in hospital medicine before entering specialist general-practice training. His prior hospital specialties included **Neurology**, as well as Cardiology, Respiratory Medicine, Endocrinology, Elderly Medicine, Renal Medicine, Infectious Diseases, Rheumatology and Palliative Care.

Brennan is not relevant because he possessed the specialist movement-disorders expertise of Counihan or Browne.

His importance is different.

He is the **pre-synthesis comparator**.

From April until late June 2017, the patient existed in Brennan's records before a neurological discharge formulation existed.

Across those encounters, the emerging object was integrated:

* abnormal and painful feet;
* abnormal gait;
* difficulty initiating movement;
* stiffness and “muscle tension”;
* increased lower-limb tone;
* deteriorating dexterity;
* declining ability to function at work;
* emerging Parkinsonian concern.

By 29 June 2017 that integrated clinical object was sent into Neurology.

The referral therefore establishes an important starting condition:

> **Neurology did not receive Parkinsonism accompanied by an unrelated podiatry complaint. It received an abnormal feet–gait–tone–movement problem requiring neurological explanation.**

## Comparator value

Brennan allows us to compare:

> **the patient before specialist formulation**

with

> **the patient after specialist formulation.**

He shows what entered the system.

The discharge summary shows what came out.

# Dr Thomas Monaghan

## Consultant Neurologist — the authorial centre of the patient-specific synthesis

Public consultant information identifies Thomas Monaghan as a Consultant in Neurology practising through the Department of Neurology at University Hospital Galway.

His publicly visible academic footprint is broader general neurology rather than the concentrated Parkin/dystonia/movement-disorders publication pattern represented by Counihan, Browne and Healy.

That distinction is important and should be preserved.

In this case, however, his importance is not primarily bibliographic.

It is **patient-specific and authorial**.

He is the treating neurologist around whom the July 2017 admission and discharge synthesis are organised.

The resulting discharge document contains sophisticated reasoning about:

* unusually young-onset Parkinsonism;
* autosomal-recessive inheritance;
* PARK2/PRKN;
* genetic counselling;
* treatment strategy;
* delaying conventional dopaminergic treatment;
* and specialist/departmental input.

At the same time, the relationship of the painful feet to the neurological disorder is left with alternatives including separation, possible peripheral neuropathy and finally:

> **“Not Clear.”**

## Monaghan's analytical position

Monaghan is therefore the transition point between:

> **bedside patient**

and

> **documentary patient.**

The decisive audit question is not whether Monaghan personally had every area of expertise demonstrated elsewhere within the Galway network.

It is:

> **What examination, reasoning and colleague input actually supported the sophisticated patient constructed in the final discharge synthesis?**

And, specifically:

> **What clinical work supported “Not Clear” after the patient had protested that his feet had not been dealt with?**

That means Counihan sits at the intersection of:

* young-onset Parkinsonism;
* Parkin/PARK2 genetics;
* dystonia;

## Patient-specific significance

The patient-specific correspondence subsequently contains Monaghan's statement:

> **“Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?”**

That wording establishes that a “Tim” participated in the treatment reasoning.

Where later clinician documentation identifies that Tim as Counihan, Counihan ceases to be merely an abstract departmental comparator and becomes part of the patient-specific professional network.

The timing still matters.

The evidence must distinguish:

* what Counihan knew before the Protest;
* what he knew after the Protest;
* when the detailed PARK2 synthesis developed;
* and what patient-specific examination or discussion his involvement generated.

## Comparator value

Counihan demonstrates that the sophistication appearing in the discharge synthesis had a plausible **local professional home**.

That makes the synthesis–examination comparison sharper.

It does not prove that Counihan examined the patient.

It asks:

> **If the final formulation drew upon expertise of this level, where is the equivalent sophistication in the examination and phenomenological analysis of the painful dystonic-looking feet?**

# Patrick Browne

## Movement-disorders expertise and the output comparator

Patrick Browne is particularly important.

His 2017 publication with Counihan places him directly inside the Galway clinical reasoning environment concerned with a young patient's **dystonic foot, abnormal gait and parkin-related juvenile Parkinsonism**.

The University of Galway research record also specifically associates Browne with that publication and Galway University Hospital.

So Browne is not simply a generic neurological nurse or later Parkinson clinician.

He represents relevant **movement-disorders expertise very close to the disputed phenotype**.

## The crucial patient-specific circumstance

The project chronology is that Browne was on holidays during this patient's hospital admission and therefore did **not** see the original presentation.

That fact should ultimately be tied to the strongest available roster, correspondence or clinical evidence.

If established, it makes Browne an unusually useful comparator.

Browne could not know the original bedside patient from personal observation.

He could know:

1. what the department documented about that patient;
2. what the discharge synthesis made portable;
3. and whatever he later learned through his own subsequent encounters.

That creates a natural experiment.

The GP knew the patient **before** the synthesis.

The treating team knew the patient **during** the admission.

Browne initially encountered the patient **through the product of the admission**.

## Why Browne is so useful

The question becomes:

> **What patient did the discharge summary transmit to someone with highly relevant movement-disorders expertise who had not witnessed the original presentation?**

If the document transmitted:

* highly developed young-onset Parkinsonian reasoning;
* PARK2/recessive genetics;
* dystonic-looking gait/lower-limb signs;
* treatment strategy;

while transmitting the relationship of the painful feet to that disease state as:

> **“Not Clear,”**

then that uncertainty becomes Browne's inherited starting position.

That creates a critical distinction between:

> **independent clinical validation**

and

> **documentary inheritance.**

Browne could later agree with the formulation.

But agreement by someone who began with the formulation is not automatically evidence that he independently validated the bedside work that generated it.

He may instead demonstrate how successfully the documentary patient reproduced itself.

The sharpest formulation is:

> **Patrick Browne could not inherit the bedside examination because he was not there. He could only inherit what the department made portable.**

# Prof Daniel G. Healy

## Neurological genetics and the external specialist comparator

Beaumont Hospital identifies Daniel Healy as a Consultant Neurologist whose scope includes **genetic disorders of the nervous system**.

His relevance is strengthened considerably by the 2013 JAMA Neurology Parkin paper.

Healy and Counihan were already collaborating on Parkin disease before this patient entered Galway Neurology.

That professional link is important because Healy subsequently became the outside specialist consulted in this patient's own case.

In September 2017 he considered the patient likely to have **PARK II**, recommended targeted genetic investigation, and commented on the patient's substantial treatment response.

## Comparator value

Healy helps divide the chronology properly.

His September opinion cannot be pushed backwards into July.

But it can be compared with the July discharge synthesis.

That permits several legitimate questions:

* How much of the PARK2 reasoning already existed before Healy?
* What did Healy independently add?
* What did Galway send to him?
* What did his specialist opinion confirm, alter or sharpen?
* What happened to his specifically PARK II formulation later?

Healy therefore functions as a **post-discharge external specialist comparator**, not as a source of retrospective justification for the July bedside assessment.

# Galway Neurology Department

## The relevant capability environment

The department itself must be treated as a separate analytical object.

Individual publication authorship cannot simply be converted into proof that every departmental clinician knew or applied the same information.

But the department's demonstrated expertise establishes what kind of clinical environment the patient entered.

By the relevant period, the Galway environment included documented work involving:

* dystonia;
* Parkinsonian disorders;
* Parkinson genetics;
* parkin/PARK2 disease;
* abnormal gait;
* freezing of gait;
* movement-state analysis;
* advanced Parkinson treatment.

Most importantly, in August 2017 the department published an exceptionally close clinical-reasoning case involving:

> **abnormal gait → dystonic foot → Parkinsonian signs → parkin-related juvenile Parkinsonism.**

And four years earlier Counihan and Healy had already collaborated on Parkin disease itself.

## What that establishes

It establishes capability.

It establishes professional connectivity.

It establishes contemporaneous familiarity with the relevant conceptual relationships.

It makes it difficult to describe the relationship between young-onset Parkinsonism, dystonia, abnormal gait and Parkin genetics as medically exotic to this environment.

## What it does not establish

It does not establish that:

* Counihan personally examined this patient;
* Browne personally saw the July presentation;
* the authors of the dystonic-foot paper applied that reasoning to this patient;
* the August 2017 publication was available before the July admission;
* every relevant examination actually took place;
* or any individual acted negligently.

Those remain separate evidential questions.

# The forensic question created by the expertise map

The argument should not be:

> **They knew everything, therefore they must have missed it.**

That overstates the evidence.

The stronger question is:

> **What clinical work earned the final formulation?**

And specifically:

> **How did a department demonstrably capable of sophisticated reasoning about young-onset Parkinsonism, Parkin genetics, dystonia, abnormal gait and dystonic foot posture reach the post-Protest conclusion “Not Clear” about the relationship between this patient's neurological syndrome and the painful abnormal feet that had formed part of his original GP referral?**

The publications do not answer that question.

They make it legitimate and precise.

# The shortest formulation

> **Brennan shows the patient who went in.
> Monaghan shows the patient the department constructed.
> Browne shows what that construction made portable.
> Healy shows what an external Parkin specialist subsequently saw.
> Counihan connects the departmental synthesis to contemporaneous expertise in both Parkin disease and dystonic-foot Parkinsonism.**

The case is therefore not built on the proposition that specialist expertise was absent.

It is built on the opposite and more difficult question:

> **The expertise was there. Where is the corresponding clinical work on the unresolved feet?**
