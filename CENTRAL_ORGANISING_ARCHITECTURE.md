# Central Organising Architecture

**Status:** CONTROLLING CASE-ORGANISATION FILE

**Function:** This file governs how the case is to be read, reconstructed, written and tested.

It does not replace the primary record, the temporal firewall, the clinical narrative, the longitudinal reconstruction, the expert instruction, the breach map or the specialist analytical files.

It tells the reader **how those materials fit together**.

Where an older formulation conflicts with this file on structure, sequencing or argumentative priority, this file controls unless the primary evidence requires correction.

---

# 1. The case in one sentence

> **An integrated feet–gait–tone–movement problem was referred to Neurology; the neurological syndrome became highly formulated, but the relationship of the painful feet to that syndrome remained unresolved; the patient protested that his feet had not been dealt with; the post-Protest discharge process then converted that unresolved clinical problem into an authoritative specialist information state marked “Not Clear”; that state shaped downstream care, while later evidence repeatedly crossed the boundary without equivalent reconstruction of the original integrated clinical object.**

That is the organising proposition.

Everything else in the case should either:

- establish one part of that proposition;
- qualify one part of it;
- test it against a competing explanation;
- identify a discrete breach threshold;
- or address causation.

---

# 2. The first rule: chronology before argument

The repository’s central discipline remains temporal.

No later knowledge may be used to make an earlier decision look more informed, more complete or more unreasonable than it actually was at the time.

The controlling sequence is:

> **integrated GP referral → inpatient bedside work → Protest → professional closure / patient departure → post-Protest discharge synthesis becomes the portable specialist state → post-discharge treatment response and dechallenge → later neurological and mechanical crossings → later review and institutional reliance.**

The following must remain separate:

## INPATIENT / PRE-PROTEST

Only information demonstrably available while the patient remained in the bedside encounter.

## PROTEST / DECISION-LIVE

The patient says in substance:

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

The Protest is an experiential challenge to the clinical work actually received.

It is not a reaction to a discharge synthesis the patient had not yet seen.

## POST-PROTEST / SYNTHESIS

The final specialist account acquires a level of neurological and neurogenetic sophistication that must be compared with the work demonstrated at the bedside.

This is where the department’s phrase:

> **“Not Clear”**

must be located.

## POST-DISCHARGE TREATMENT

September–October 2017 treatment response and withdrawal create new evidence.

This evidence did not exist at the Protest threshold.

## RETROSPECTIVE / REOPENING

April 2018 and later clinician-authored observations generate further tests of the inherited boundary.

## LATER REVIEW / GOVERNANCE

Complaint, review and governance material must be treated as later institutional uses of the inherited record, not as evidence of what was known at the bedside.

# 3. The Protest is the phase change

The Protest is not merely another event in the timeline.

It changes the status of everything that follows.

Before the Protest:

> the assessment is still capable of being extended, corrected or completed.

At the Protest:

> the patient expressly identifies the feet as an unfinished part of the presenting problem.

After the Protest:

> closure occurs in a changed information environment.

The final synthesis must therefore be read as a **post-Protest documentary act**.

The central transition is:

> **unfinished bedside problem → express notice → closure → authoritative specialist representation of the unresolved problem.**

That is the phase change.

---

# 4. “Not Clear” is not a neutral free-floating fact

“Not Clear” must always be read through its provenance.

It is:

> **the post-Protest voice of the department dealing with the residual foot question that entered Neurology through the GP referral.**

It is not:

- the GP’s uncertainty;
- the patient’s uncertainty;
- a neutral statement detached from the work done;
- or proof that adequate investigation necessarily occurred.

The correct question is:

> **What clinical work earned “Not Clear”?**

The crucial distinction is:

> **residual uncertainty after adequate assessment**

versus

> **uncertainty produced by insufficient investigation.**

If the surviving record ultimately supports the second state, then the documentary problem becomes:

> **an insufficiency of examination was transmitted downstream as an insufficiency of medical knowledge.**

That proposition is central to the case.

---

# 5. The synthesis–examination differential

The final discharge synthesis is an internal comparator.

The case should not be framed as:

> the patient was complicated, therefore uncertainty was inevitable.

The correct comparator is:

> **what the department ultimately proved capable of constructing**

against

> **what the surviving record shows was actually tested while the patient remained available.**

The controlling formulation remains:

> **The synthesis may have outrun the examination.**

More formally:

> **synthetic completeness may have exceeded evidential completeness.**

The question is not whether sophisticated PRKN/PARK2 reasoning was wrong.

It is:

> **How did the service reach high-resolution neurological and neurogenetic construction while the disputed painful foot–lower-limb relationship remained documented at much lower resolution?**

The strong expert question is:

> **What work, and whose work, earned the sophistication of the final formulation?**

And specifically:

> **What work earned the post-Protest treatment of the feet as “Not Clear”?**

# 6. The actor-state model

The same discharge synopsis has different effects on different people.

This distinction is now centrally controlling.

## 6.1 Aaron Brennan — input comparator + downstream information state

Brennan knows the patient before specialist synthesis.

He sends an integrated problem into Neurology:

> **feet + gait + tone + movement + deteriorating function.**

After the specialist admission, the discharge synthesis places him into a new information state.

He is entitled to understand “Not Clear” as the result of a specialist investigation of the question he referred.

He cannot see from the phrase itself whether:

> **Neurology investigated adequately and remained uncertain**

or

> **Neurology remained uncertain because the relevant work had not been completed.**

Brennan therefore demonstrates both:

- the **input state** of the case;
- and the **downstream informational effect** of the discharge synthesis.

He also retains an important protection: he remembers the patient he originally referred.

---

## 6.2 Thomas Monaghan — founding encounter + behaviour / commitment state

Monaghan is categorically different from the downstream actors.

He does not learn the admission from the discharge summary.

He possesses the underlying encounter.

He knows:

- the referral;
- the patient;
- the bedside presentation;
- the painful feet;
- the abnormal gait;
- the dystonic-looking lower limb;
- the course of the admission;
- and the Protest.

For Monaghan, the discharge synthesis is therefore not primarily an information state.

It is a:

> **behaviour state**

and a:

> **commitment state.**

He moves from:

> **knowing the patient in the encounter**

to

> **choosing the representation of the patient that will leave the encounter and govern downstream understanding.**

The controlling distinction is:

> **The summary changes knowledge downstream; it changes commitment upstream.**

Later conduct by Monaghan must therefore be analysed as conduct occurring after an existing commitment to the founding formulation, not as though he were merely another reader of the record.

---

## 6.3 Patrick Browne — movement-disorders expertise + downstream information state

Browne possesses highly relevant movement-disorders expertise.

That expertise includes the clinical neighbourhood of:

- dystonia;
- abnormal gait;
- young-onset Parkinsonism;
- Parkinson genetics;
- foot phenomenology;
- treatment response.

But expertise does not create retrospective access to a bedside encounter he did not witness.

The important founding fact is not a holiday theory.

The relevant record-level proposition is:

> **Parkinson’s Disease ANP input was still pending in the July synthesis.**

Browne therefore begins downstream of the founding bedside encounter.

His initial neurological patient is necessarily mediated by:

- the discharge synthesis;
- the consultant formulation;
- handover;
- and whatever he later learns independently.

Thus:

> **Browne’s initial uncertainty may be inherited.**

His responsibility, if any, begins only when later direct encounters give him enough independent information to test or reopen the inherited frame.

Shortest form:

> **Browne could not inherit the bedside examination. He could inherit only what the department made portable, until later direct evidence gave him something new.**

---

## 6.4 Timothy Counihan — internal expertise + patient-specific decision environment

Counihan is not merely an abstract departmental comparator.

His real-world expertise links:

- PRKN/PARK2;
- young-onset Parkinsonism;
- dystonia;
- gait;
- movement disorders.

His prior professional connection with Daniel Healy in Parkin disease and his Galway work with Patrick Browne show that the relevant expertise existed in the actual professional environment.

His patient-specific significance increases where the record identifies “Tim” in the treatment decision to defer levodopa.

The controlling question is:

> **When did Counihan’s expertise enter this patient-specific case, what information was available to him at that point, and what clinical work did that expertise generate?**

Do not infer from expertise alone that he examined the patient’s feet.

---

## 6.5 Daniel Healy — external specialist + independent post-discharge state

Healy begins as an external specialist receiving a referred information state.

Once he personally examines the patient in September 2017, he creates a new independent clinical state.

His opinion that the patient probably had PARK II belongs there.

Healy must not be projected backwards into the July bedside state.

His value is as a transition:

> **inherited referral state → independent specialist reassessment.**

# 7. The expertise argument

The expertise argument is not:

> **They knew everything, therefore they must have missed it.**

That is too crude.

The proper argument is:

> **The relevant expertise demonstrably existed; parts of it demonstrably entered this patient’s final synthesis and treatment network; the unresolved question is what clinical work shows that equivalent sophistication was applied to the painful feet–gait–tone relationship before it was exported as “Not Clear.”**

The three principal external/public comparators are:

## 2013 — Parkin disease

Counihan + Healy

Shows:

> pre-existing Parkin/PARK2 expertise and professional connection.

## 2015 — Parkinson genetics

Browne + Counihan

Shows:

> Galway movement-disorders / Parkinson-genetics capability, including PRKN.

## 2017 — _A demure teenager and her dystonic foot_

Browne + Counihan + Galway Neurology

Shows:

> contemporaneous Galway reasoning across abnormal gait, foot dystonia, young-onset parkinsonism and parkin-related disease.

The proper use of these materials is capability and professional context.

They do not prove who knew what at the bedside unless patient-specific evidence establishes that separately.

---

# 8. The central asymmetry

The case contains a major asymmetry:

> **the neurological and genetic formulation became highly resolved while the originating painful-foot relationship remained unresolved.**

Or:

> **high-resolution genetics travelled outward while the feet travelled outward as “Not Clear.”**

That asymmetry is not itself proof of breach.

It creates the question:

> **What work earned the difference in resolution?**

This should be one of the first questions a reader encounters.

---

# 9. The integrated clinical object

The original referral object must never disappear from the case.

It is approximately:

> **painful abnormal feet + abnormal gait + abnormal tone / movement disorder → determine their relationship.**

Later services may legitimately decompose the problem into:

- Parkinsonism;
- dystonia;
- Podiatry pathology;
- neuropathic pain;
- OFF-state fluctuation;
- medication management.

But local decomposition does not automatically resolve the original relationship.

The controlling systems question is:

> **Who owned the arrows?**

That means:

> **motor state ↔ gait ↔ loading ↔ structural lesion ↔ pain ↔ treatment state.**

The key proposition is:

> **Neurology did not have to own every foot lesion. It may nevertheless have had to own the unresolved neurological relationship.**

And:

> **A problem can have several competent service owners and still have no owner of the whole.**

---

# 10. Object preservation

The case is not simply about whether later evidence was ignored.

Much of it was not ignored.

The deeper question is whether the original integrated clinical object remained alive as an object of inquiry.

The controlling formulation is:

> **The unresolved relationship did not disappear from the patient. It may have disappeared as an object of inquiry.**

That creates the reconstruction test:

> **Where is the whole reassembled?**

This is different from the missing-transition test.

## Missing transition

Example:

> **probable / suspected PARK II → [what changed?] → categorical iPD**

## Missing reconstruction

Example:

> **integrated feet–gait–tone presentation → partition → repeated cross-boundary evidence → [where is the whole reassembled?] → later continuity claim**

Both tests are controlling.

---

# 11. Later evidence must be read as boundary testing

Later evidence must not be used to manufacture hindsight.

Its proper function is to test the durability of the exported boundary.

The key later sequence is:

> **post-discharge treatment response → withdrawal / dechallenge → rapid foot-state change → recurrent focal mechanical pathology → GP reintegration → OFF-state / dystonic phenomena → toe curling → dopaminergic pain response.**

No single observation has to prove one mechanism.

The correct question is:

> **What was each observation capable of changing, and what did it actually change?**

The repository’s controlling formulation is:

> **selective utilisation without integrative revision.**

Or:

> **local updating, global non-revision.**

Shortest:

> **The system kept learning facts. The expert question is whether it adequately learned what those facts meant together.**

---

# 12. April 2018 is the independent clinical backstop

The case must not stand or fall entirely on July 2017 or the Protest.

The later treatment-state evidence creates a separate threshold.

The independent question is:

> **Even if the July 2017 “Not Clear” position was initially reasonable, once the treating neurologist recorded that withdrawal of procyclidine was followed by rapid return of the focal outside-left-foot phenomenon, did reasonable neurological practice require that original relationship to be reopened and investigated? If not, why not?**

This question survives even if:

- the Protest were disputed;
- July uncertainty were initially reasonable;
- Podiatry were appropriate;
- Parkinsonism were correctly diagnosed;
- and the structural foot lesion were genuinely mechanical.

That is why April 2018 is the strongest independent backstop.

---

# 13. Mixed mechanism is not a defence to integration

The case should never require proof that all foot pathology was neurological.

The real structural pathology must remain visible.

The correct formulation is:

> **structural pathology does not establish structural independence.**

The working mechanisms may include:

- mechanically independent pathology;
- neurologically influenced loading;
- dystonia / tone;
- gait disturbance;
- altered posture;
- pain modulation;
- mixed mechanism.

The expert question is not:

> **Which one mechanism explains everything?**

It is:

> **Was the relationship among the mechanisms adequately investigated and owned?**

---

# 14. The information architecture argument

The founding synthesis does more than record the patient.

It creates categories into which future observations are likely to fall.

Once the foot is made compatible with:

> **separate / neuropathic / Podiatry / “Not Clear”**

later observations can acquire local meanings:

> later corn → Podiatry evidence

> later burning pain → pain / neuropathic evidence

> later OFF deterioration → Parkinson fluctuation evidence

> later toe curling → dystonia evidence

> later Sinemet response → pain-modulation evidence

Each local interpretation may be individually reasonable.

The controlling question is:

> **Did any of those observations regain enough weight to change what the original foot problem was understood to be?**

This is why the repository says:

> **The first synthesis may have priced the evidential value of everything that came after it.**

And:

> **The existing information architecture may have determined what later evidence was allowed to mean.**

These are structural hypotheses, not allegations of motive.

---

# 15. Clinical lock-in

Clinical lock-in must be understood structurally before it is understood psychologically.

The controlling model is:

> **integrated clinical object → decomposition → specialty-specific objects → local updating → no integrative owner → original relationship no longer treated as a live object → global non-revision.**

This does not require:

- stubbornness;
- dishonesty;
- concealment;
- conspiracy;
- or conscious bias.

The case should therefore prefer:

> **information-architecture lock-in**

over any unsupported psychological allegation.

The observable test is:

> **Did later evidence cause the founding formulation to be reconsidered, or did the founding formulation principally govern how later evidence was interpreted?**

---

# 16. Institutional parallax

The same unresolved clinical relationship changes institutional form over time:

> **integrated clinical question → bedside uncertainty → Protest → professional closure → final documentary state → Podiatry problem → inherited neurological history → complaint evidence → governance object.**

This is:

> **institutional parallax.**

The human centre is:

> **The body remained unresolved while the institution progressively resolved the record.**

The critical systems question is:

> **At which transitions was the underlying relationship independently reconstructed rather than merely translated into the language of the next function?**

---

# 17. Documentary resilience

Once finalised, the discharge synthesis becomes documentary infrastructure.

The controlling distinction is:

> **visible reasoning is not the same as demonstrated clinical work.**

A sophisticated document can create an appearance of thoroughness.

Later clinicians and reviewers may then inherit that appearance.

The documentary sequence is:

> **clinical work → authoritative synthesis → downstream continuity → retrospective review → validation → governance reliance.**

The question is:

> **Did later review independently go beneath the synthesis and test whether the medicine actually done matched the medicine the synthesis made it appear had been done?**

This is a provenance question.

It is not automatic circularity.

---

# 18. Continuity of what?

The case must always distinguish broad diagnostic continuity from specific aetiological continuity.

The historical sequence includes:

> **recessive / PRKN-PARK2 reasoning → Healy: probably PARK II → later broader Parkinson’s / iPD continuity.**

The important question is:

> **What evidence or reasoning moved the formulation from the specific earlier state to the later broader state?**

And:

> **Where is that transition documented?**

The controlling phrase is:

> **Healy’s authority survived; Healy’s proposition did not.**

This is a specific example of the broader missing-transition problem.

# 20. Discrete breach architecture

One clinical history does not mean one breach.

The controlling map remains:

## A. July 2017 bedside assessment / formulation

> **Was the feet–gait–tone relationship adequately assessed while the patient remained available?**

## B. Protest / decision after express notice

> **Once the patient said the feet had not been dealt with, what did reasonable practice require before closure?**

## C. Final discharge communication

> **Did the exported summary fairly represent the provenance of “Not Clear” and the decision process that produced it?**

## D. Inter-specialty handoff / reintegration

> **Was Podiatry given a sufficiently defined neurological question and return mechanism?**

## E. 2017–2018 failure to reopen

> **Did treatment-state evidence require reconsideration of the founding boundary?**

## F. Later threshold-specific failures to update

Each must have:

- a date;
- an information state;
- a required response;
- and an independent expert basis.

## G. 2023 safety / reassessment

This remains separate.

It must not be used to prove 2017 by analogy.

---

# 21. Causation must remain separate

For every alleged breach:

1. What was known then?
2. What should reasonable practice have required?
3. What was omitted?
4. What would competent care probably have done?
5. What would that probably have shown?
6. What management would probably have followed?
7. Which later harms would probably have been avoided or reduced?

Never allow:

> **breach → therefore all later harm**

to become the structure.

Each breach requires its own counterfactual.

# 22. The competing-model test

Every major section should preserve the strongest competing explanation.

## Model A — ordinary complexity and legitimate decomposition

- Parkinsonism was real.
- Structural foot disease was real.
- Podiatry was appropriate.
- Uncertainty was reasonable.
- Later treatment effects were interpreted appropriately.
- Different services properly managed different components.
- No global reconstruction was clinically required.

## Model B — founding partition and resilient information architecture

- the original integrated relationship was insufficiently resolved;
- the Protest identified the incompleteness;
- closure nevertheless occurred;
- the final synthesis exported apparently considered uncertainty;
- downstream actors inherited that state;
- later evidence repeatedly crossed the boundary;
- evidence was used locally without equivalent reconstruction of the whole;
- the founding formulation acquired documentary and institutional resilience.

The controlling question is:

> **Which model explains the documented sequence with fewer unsupported or invisible transitions?**

This is one of the strongest ways to make the case intelligible without overclaiming.

# 23. How the reader should read the case

The reader should not begin by asking:

> **Was the foot pain Parkinsonian?**

That is too small and too retrospective.

The reader should begin with:

> **What problem did Brennan send to Neurology?**

Then:

> **What did Neurology actually examine?**

Then:

> **What remained unresolved?**

Then:

> **What did the patient identify at the Protest?**

Then:

> **What did the department finally say?**

Then:

> **What did “Not Clear” cause downstream recipients reasonably to understand had been investigated?**

Then:

> **What later evidence crossed the boundary?**

Then:

> **What did each new observation actually change?**

Then:

> **Where was the original integrated object reconstructed?**

Then:

> **Who owned the arrows?**

Only after those questions should the reader ask:

> **Was there breach?**

And only after breach:

> **What harm probably followed from that particular breach?**

# 25. Mandatory drafting rules

Every future analytical document should obey these rules.

## Rule 1

State the information threshold before making the proposition.

## Rule 2

Do not let the final synthesis travel backwards.

## Rule 3

Do not let post-discharge treatment travel backwards into the admission.

## Rule 4

Do not make the Protest an objection to knowledge the patient did not possess.

## Rule 5

Do not infer bedside application from departmental expertise alone.

## Rule 6

Do not infer structural independence from structural pathology.

## Rule 7

Do not treat Podiatry appropriateness as proof that Neurology had completed its integrative task.

## Rule 8

Do not treat downstream repetition as independent validation without testing provenance.

## Rule 9

Do not treat later reconsideration of current symptoms as proof that the original historical relationship was reconstructed.

## Rule 10

Do not collapse all later evidence into one mechanism.

## Rule 11

Do not collapse all possible breaches into one continuing negligence allegation.

## Rule 12

Keep breach and causation separate.

# 27. The final controlling formulation

> **The case should be read as the history of an integrated clinical object that entered Neurology, was only partly resolved at the bedside, was expressly identified by the patient as unfinished at the Protest, and was then translated into a sophisticated post-Protest specialist synthesis in which the neurological disease was strongly formulated but the feet remained “Not Clear.” That synthesis created an authoritative information state for the patient, the GP, the movement-disorders nurse and later clinicians, while for the treating neurologist it represented a behavioural commitment to the formulation being exported. The later record repeatedly generated neurological, mechanical and treatment-state evidence capable of bearing on the original relationship. Much of that evidence was clinically used, but the central audit question is whether it was ever permitted to reconstruct the original integrated object rather than being absorbed into the categories created by the founding synthesis. The case therefore turns not on proving that every foot lesion was Parkinsonian, but on whether the neurological–foot relationship was adequately investigated, owned, transmitted, reopened and integrated at the successive information thresholds where reasonable practice required it.**
