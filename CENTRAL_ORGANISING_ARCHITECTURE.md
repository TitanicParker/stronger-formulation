# 1. The case in one sentence

> **Neurology received an integrated feet–gait–tone–movement problem and identified a severe movement disorder expressed through a dystonic-looking lower limb, but the surviving record does not demonstrate a directed investigation sufficient to show how that motor disorder related to the painful abnormal feet before the final synthesis stated that the foot symptoms “may have been completely separate,” reinforced that separation by recording that the patient had “markedly divorced” foot pain from bodily tension, and operationalised the allocation through Podiatry; the Podiatry allocation later ended, but the conceptual partition survived inside Neurology, was repeatedly crossed by later foot–movement evidence, and ultimately became an express hospital governance state.**

---

# 2. The central correction

The **relationship** was unresolved.

The **segregation** was imposed.

The founding sequence is:

> **integrated problem → components detected → relationship insufficiently demonstrated as investigated → “may have been completely separate” → “markedly divorced” → “Not clear” → Podiatry → authoritative specialist allocation.**

Shortest form:

> **“Completely separate” supplied the partition.  
> “Markedly divorced” supplied apparent patient-side support.  
> “Not clear” preserved epistemic uncertainty.  
> Podiatry supplied the initial operational destination.**

# 4. The explicit negative examination audit

Do **not** turn the user's 50-item audit into an allegation that 50 mandatory tests were omitted.

Use only the items for which the complete record has **no adequate documented counterpart**.

On the current conservative audit, 20 of the 50 proposed examination/question domains are not found as adequately documented anywhere in the complete record:

1. foot pain specifically characterised **at rest**;
2. **great-toe extension** as a dystonic posture;
3. directed assessment of **ankle twisting / dystonic torsion**;
4. specific **foot posture while sitting**;
5. **walking backward** examination;
6. **heel walking**;
7. **toe walking**;
8. **tandem walking**;
9. **heel-strike** assessment;
10. **Achilles reflex** examination;
11. a true **ankle-clonus test**;
12. **plantar response / Babinski**;
13. formal **light-touch sensory examination of the feet**;
14. formal **pinprick sensory examination of the feet**;
15. **vibration at the great toe**;
16. **joint-position / proprioceptive testing**;
17. foot **capillary refill / temperature / colour** assessment;
18. directed **focal bony tenderness** examination;
19. **plantar-fascia tenderness** examination;
20. **Achilles-tendon tenderness** examination.

This is a documentary negative only.

> **No adequate counterpart found in the record does not prove the examination was not performed.**

Its significance is comparative. The record is detailed about adjacent neurological work and yet does not preserve these particular discriminating examinations.

Do not count as “missing” matters that are documented later, even if absent in July. Inversion, eversion, plantarflexion, dorsiflexion and lower-limb power are later expressly tested; dynamic walking and turning are later observed; callus / pressure-point mechanics are extensively documented by Podiatry. The audit must stay conservative.

The important concentration of the explicit negatives is in three areas:

## A. Dynamic dystonia / gait characterisation

Great-toe extension, ankle twisting, sitting posture, backward walking, heel walking, toe walking, tandem gait and heel strike.

## B. Peripheral-neurological discrimination

Achilles reflex, ankle clonus, plantar response, light touch, pinprick, vibration and proprioception.

## C. Directed local mechanical / vascular examination

Rest pain, capillary refill / temperature / colour, focal bony tenderness, plantar-fascia tenderness and Achilles-tendon tenderness.

That concentration matters because the final synthesis itself entertained both **complete separation** and **peripheral neuropathy**.

The expert question is not whether every one of these 20 manoeuvres was mandatory. It is:

> **Given the alternatives being advanced, what examination was reasonably necessary to distinguish them, and where is the contemporaneous evidence that such an examination occurred?**

# 5. The neuropathy branch must show its work

The final synthesis states that the leg / foot symptoms including corns, callouses and burning:

> **“may have been completely separate”**

or perhaps represented a secondary peripheral neuropathy, and concludes:

> **“Not clear.”**

The explicit negative audit is especially important here because the complete record does not disclose an adequate formal foot sensory examination covering light touch, pinprick, vibration or joint position, nor a documented Achilles reflex, plantar response or true ankle-clonus test.

That does not prove neuropathy was impossible.

It creates the narrower question:

> **What positive clinical evidence earned peripheral neuropathy as an explanatory branch?**

And then:

> **Was “Not clear” the result of an adequate discriminating examination, or the documentary disposition of a relationship that had not been sufficiently interrogated?**

---

# 6. “Markedly divorced”: not identical is not independent

The final synthesis records that the patient had:

> **“in his own mind - markedly divorced”**

his bodily / leg tension from the corns, callouses and pains in the feet.

The underlying history was that foot pain changed with standing / occupational exposure while bodily tension persisted.

That may establish phenomenological distinction. It does not by itself establish mechanistic independence.

The expert question remains:

> **Did “markedly divorced” transform the patient's distinction between experiences into apparent support for separation between mechanisms?**

---

# 7. Detection versus relationship investigation

The lower-limb abnormality was not missed. The record shows striking gait, pseudo-footdrop, dystonic posture, videoed gait, severe tone / rigidity, bradykinesia and structural foot abnormalities.

The criticism, if expert evidence supports it, is therefore not failure to detect a sign.

It is:

> **A sign was detected and neurologically characterised, but the record does not demonstrate a comparably directed investigation of what that sign meant for the painful structural feet before separation.**

# 10. The synthesis–examination differential

The final document is highly sophisticated in neurological and neurogenetic reasoning.

Use that sophistication as an internal comparator, not as proof of timing.

> **The synthesis may have outrun the examination.**

> **Synthetic completeness is not evidential completeness.**

The sharper question is:

> **What clinical work earned “may have been completely separate,” peripheral neuropathy, “Not clear,” and the practical separation of the feet?**

# 11. Podiatry and ownership of the arrows

Podiatry was appropriate for genuine structural pathology.

The issue is whether Podiatry was given a neurological question, a defined relationship to dystonia / tone / gait / loading, a feedback loop, a return criterion, or a clear statement that Neurology retained ownership of the relationship.

> **Neurology did not have to own every foot lesion. It may nevertheless have had to own the arrows.**

Meaning:

> **motor state ↔ gait ↔ loading ↔ structural lesion ↔ pain ↔ treatment state.**

# 12. Institutional lifespan of the segregation

## Phase 1 — 2017: founding clinical segregation

Neurology receives the integrated problem, detects the neurological syndrome and dystonic-looking lower limb, and exports the feet through the possible-separation / “Not clear” / Podiatry structure.

## Phase 2 — September 2017 to March 2019: operational segregation

Neurology acquires dynamic motor / treatment-state information while Podiatry acquires focal structural / loading information.

> **What mechanism existed for those complementary truths to meet?**

## Phase 3 — after 25 March 2019: allocation disappears, conceptual partition survives

Podiatry identifies **25 March 2019 as the last attendance**.

Brennan's March 2019 referral had already re-linked feet, increased tone, abnormal gait and Parkinson's disease.

Thereafter Neurology directly reacquires foot evidence: Parkinsonian foot throw, sensitivity / excessive movement, medication-state information, morning warm-up, OFF dystonia / pain, toe curling and dopaminergic pain response.

> **After March 2019, the feet are no longer elsewhere; the separation is.**

## Phase 4 — 2025 to January 2026: governance adoption

The historical integration issue is expressly put before the hospital through complaint / QPS / PAR / SIMT processes. The institutional question becomes:

> **Did governance go beneath the inherited clinical architecture and ask what work had originally justified the separation?**

## Phase 5 — February 2026: retrospective comparator

Later genetics confirms two pathogenic Parkin variants and attributes Parkinsonism and dystonia to compound-heterozygous Parkin mutations.

This does not prove that historic foot pain was all dystonia and must not be projected backwards.

It sharpens the longitudinal question:

> **How many times did feet + dystonia + gait + treatment-state interaction enter the record without the founding feet–movement relationship being reconstructed?**

# 17. Duty, breach, harm and causation

## Duty

> **Reasonable Neurology had to investigate and retain ownership of the relationship sufficiently to avoid treating the disabling feet as clinically independent before there was an adequate basis for doing so.**

## Founding breach

> **If expert evidence supports it, the founding breach is segregation before sufficient discriminating relationship-directed examination is demonstrated.**

## Decision-after-notice breach

> **The Protest supplies express notice while correction remains possible.**

## Later reopening breach

> **Later cross-boundary evidence may independently require reconstruction even if July care was initially reasonable.**

## Harm

Keep direct clinical harm separate from primary-care information harm.

## Causation

> **competent assessment → probable finding → probable avoidance / reversal of segregation → probable management change → probable effect on motor state / loading / pain / structural pathology → probable reduction in incremental morbidity.**

Do not attribute the whole later disease burden to breach.
