What this is

A way into the record, not a second record.

This repository organises a large clinical and documentary record around a simple unresolved question: why did the feet hurt so badly, and what did the neurological problem have to do with it?

The pages and working documents below serve different purposes. They are not interchangeable. The canonical clinical record is the evidential backstop; the Case Register controls the presentation distinctions between quotations, facts, propositions, arguments and expert questions; the remaining analytical and expert documents stay derived from those layers.

The later human question They gave him neurological tablets. His feet got much better. When they stopped the tablets, the foot problem came back quickly. What did that require Neurology to reconsider?

Short orientation

Keep the chronology visible.

The point of a timeline here is not to decide the case. It is to stop later knowledge from silently becoming earlier knowledge.

  1. Before Neurology

    Disabling foot pain, abnormal feet, abnormal gait, stiffness and movement difficulty were still part of one unresolved clinical picture.

  2. July 2017

    Neurology identified a severe movement disorder and a strikingly abnormal gait. The source of the foot pain remained unresolved, while the feet could be treated as a possibly separate problem.

  3. Oct 2017 → Apr 2018

    During a planned withdrawal of neurological medication, the foot problem was reported and later documented as returning quickly. That created a new reason to ask whether the feet were really separate.

  4. 2019 onward

    Foot, gait and treatment interactions recur in the later record. The evidence is mixed; later reconsideration is part of the history.

  5. 2025 → 2026

    Governance material and later specialist or genetic information arrive. They may matter later, but they must not be imported backwards into what was known in 2017.

Evidential discipline

Simplify the language. Keep the provenance.

This site is an interface to the case. It does not acquire evidential authority merely because it is easier to read.

  • A quotation is not automatically a fact about mechanism.
  • Allegation does not become fact by repetition.
  • Inference does not become a clinical finding.
  • An expert question is not an expert conclusion.
  • Later evidence does not become earlier knowledge.
  • Patient-authored interpretation does not become an independent clinical finding.
  • Where derived material and the canonical record differ, return to the canonical record.

For the documentary argument, continue to Leave It At That. For the patient's reason for bringing the case, read Why This Is the Case We Are Taking.
