# 0. The controlling mechanism — the founding synthesis organises the uncertainty

The deepest proposition in the case is not that one diagnosis was missed.

It is that the 2017 specialist synthesis may have changed the **information state** in which the pain travelled thereafter.

The problem originally entered Neurology as an open relationship:

> **painful abnormal feet + abnormal gait + increased tone/stiffness + movement difficulty + toe clawing**

Neurology then identified severe Parkinsonism and a lower limb suggestive of dystonic posture rather than true foot drop.

The unresolved question was:

> **What does the neurological disorder have to do with the painful feet?**

The later synthesis did not answer that relationship. It allowed the foot/leg symptoms to be:

> **“completely separate”**

or possibly neuropathic, before concluding:

> **“Not clear.”**

That distinction is load-bearing.

There is a difference between transmitting:

> **We have not established how the neurological disorder and the painful feet relate.**

and transmitting:

> **Parkinsonism is established; the foot problem may be separate; its cause is not clear.**

The second formulation gives every later clinician a starting model.

If the relationship-directed work was incomplete, later clinicians may inherit **specialist uncertainty as though it were the product of adequate prior investigation**.

Then each new fact can be explained locally:

- structural disease;
- neuropathic pain;
- OFF pain;
- dystonia;
- medication effect;
- toe curling;
- loading;
- pregabalin response;
- levodopa response.

Any of those local explanations may contain truth.

The longitudinal danger is different:

> **the label changes while the founding relationship is never globally reconstructed.**

That is the plumbing of the case.

> **The founding synthesis did not merely record uncertainty. It organised the uncertainty.**

> **The problem is not that information disappeared. The problem is the shape in which it travelled.**

> **“Not clear” can become self-reproducing when every later clinician starts downstream of the original synthesis rather than from the original question.**

This is not itself proof of negligence.

It is the controlling mechanism the three arguments test.

- **Argument One** asks whether the information state was adequately earned in the first place.
- **Argument Two** asks what changes if the patient expressly challenged that state before discharge and the challenge did not travel.
- **Argument Three** asks what reasonable care required when years later the same pain had become a documented safety problem and Neurology itself recorded an explanatory misalignment.

The master longitudinal question is therefore:

> **At each transition, did new evidence merely receive another local label, or did reasonable care require reconstruction of the original feet–gait–movement–pain relationship?**

> **Unresolved source of disabling pain**

Everything else is subordinate to those two transformations and the information mechanism above them.

The master question is:

# 2. Argument One — Founding Separation

**This survives if the Protest is rejected.**

The patient entered Neurology with painful abnormal feet, abnormal gait, increased tone/stiffness, movement-initiation difficulty, toe clawing and declining function.

Neurology correctly identified severe young-onset Parkinsonism. It also recorded a striking gait and a lower-limb appearance suggestive of dystonic posture rather than true foot drop.

Structural pathology was real. Podiatry was appropriate.

But structural pathology is not structural independence.

The surviving record does not clearly show the work that established how the abnormal neurological lower limb related to the painful structural feet before the feet were allowed to sit as something that **“may have been completely separate”**, possibly neuropathic, and **“Not clear.”**

The question is:

> **They saw the abnormal neurological walk. They knew the feet hurt. What work earned the separation?**

And then:

> **What did that separation cause later clinicians reasonably to assume had already been investigated?**

That is the strongest document-only case.

# 3. Argument Two — Protest Fork

**Conditional on establishing the Protest as evidence.**

The patient says, in substance:

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

The Protest does not prove dystonia, negligence or mechanism.

It does something more exact:

> **It converts possible omission into express notice while the original phenotype is still available for correction.**

Once the Protest is established, the question becomes:

> **What did the service choose to do once the patient had pointed directly at the unfinished source of disabling pain?**

The available futures included re-examination, limited targeted review, senior/movement-disorders input, a defined Neurology–Podiatry question, active uncertainty, reassurance, retained ownership, planned return, documented disagreement, or simply preserving the Protest in the portable record.

No claim is made that one particular response was automatically mandatory.

The point is that many responsible futures were still open.

> **The Protest is the widest point in the future.**

If it occurred and did not travel in the specialist account:

> **its omission removes the fork from history.**

It also matters to the information mechanism.

Without the Protest, a downstream clinician sees specialist uncertainty.

With the Protest, the true information state may instead have been:

> **specialist uncertainty that the patient contemporaneously said had not been adequately worked through.**

Those are not equivalent.

Under Irish law, the spotlight is therefore not motive. It is:

> **information available → choices available → course actually followed → professional standard → consequence.**

# 4. Argument Three — Safety and Misalignment, 2023–2025

**This does not depend on the Protest.**

By 2023 the pain problem has changed status.

It is no longer only a source-of-pain question.

It is a safety question.

## 1 February 2023 — direct patient notice

The patient emails Thomas Monaghan, Patrick Browne and Aaron Brennan.

He calls foot pain:

> **“The hardest part of the entire experience.”**

He says the residual pain is:

> **“absolutely intolerable though not unbearable.”**

Then:

> **“I don’t think I could live with it anymore. It is perfect torture.”**

This proves direct transmission to the named addresses. It does not by itself prove suicidal intent or when each recipient read it.

## 26 March 2023 — GP escalation

The GP sends an urgent Neurology referral:

> **“causing death wish.”**

> **“I am worried about him.”**

> **“Despite his facade, I don’t think he is doing well. I’d be grateful for your review before we get into trouble here.”**

This is clinician-authored safety escalation.

## 28 March 2023 — nursing corroboration

Neurology receives:

> **“debilitating pain”**

and:

> **“no quality of life.”**

## Later 2023 — explanatory conflict becomes explicit

The neurologist records a:

> **“misalignment”**

and says it would be a fair characterisation of the patient’s view that the clinician held a:

> **“firm belief that everything has to be explainable by the Parkinson’s”**

while describing the patient’s contrary concerns about the feet as:

> **“very reasonable and justifiable.”**

The same note says it would be **“embarrassing”** if there were another cause, while expressly saying that getting the diagnosis right mattered more than being personally right.

This is not proof of bad faith or legal anchoring.

It proves something more useful:

> **the explanatory disagreement itself had become clinically visible.**

That is where the controlling mechanism becomes explicit inside the medical record itself: the patient and clinician are no longer merely carrying different symptoms; they are carrying different models of what the persistent foot problem means.

## 2025 — life-tolerability language inside Neurology’s own record

The Neurology note records:

> **“murder in the feet”**

> **the first 150 footsteps are “torture”**

and:

> **“I won’t live with it.”**

The question is:

> **What structured safeguarding response, if any, corresponded to the life-safety signal itself?**

The reviewed corpus shows substantial neurological and pain management.

It does **not yet show a documented safeguarding bridge** equivalent in visibility to the warning language.

That is a documentary negative, not proof that no undocumented response occurred.

The expert question is:

> **What did reasonable care require once severe pain, life-tolerability language, an urgent GP death-wish warning and acknowledged explanatory misalignment existed at the same time?**

# 5. Why Argument Three changes the whole case

The 2023 crisis is not a damages appendix.

It does four things:

1. gives documented notice independent of the Protest;
2. joins unresolved pain to explicit safety concern;
3. joins safety concern to an acknowledged dispute about the explanatory model;
4. creates a second decision-after-notice case.

It also exposes the information mechanism prospectively.

By then, several local explanations have existed, but the record itself acknowledges that the governing model remains contested.

So even if the Protest is rejected entirely, the case still asks:

> **By 2023 the service had documentary notice that the pain had become dangerous to the patient’s ability to endure life with it, while Neurology itself recognised a material explanatory misalignment. What did reasonable care require then — another local explanation, or reconstruction of the problem?**

The Protest makes the case earlier.

The 2023 documents make the mechanism harder to deny.

# 6. Democratic case-selection and teaching test

The patient should be able to explain the case to family and friends without asking them to become neurologists or lawyers.

Teach it in six moves:

1. **What entered Neurology?** — painful feet, abnormal gait, tone, movement difficulty.
2. **What did Neurology find?** — severe Parkinsonism plus a dystonic-looking lower limb.
3. **What happened to the pain question?** — possibly separate / neuropathy / Not clear.
4. **What did that synthesis make the next clinician inherit?** — not the original open relationship, but an already organised uncertainty.
5. **What changes if the Protest is true?** — the service was expressly told the painful problem remained unresolved while other futures were still open.
6. **What does 2023 independently prove?** — direct intolerability language, GP death-wish escalation, no quality of life, and acknowledged explanatory misalignment.

Then ask:

> **What case do you think this is?**

Their answer is not evidence of breach or causation.

Its purpose is to test whether the architecture teaches the evidence honestly and whether a non-specialist can see where the true evidential hinge lies.

# 7. Record-absorption rule

Every new medical record must answer at least one of these questions:

1. Does it change the founding integrated presentation?
2. Does it show the relationship-directed work actually occurred?
3. Does it earn separation?
4. Does it weaken separation?
5. Does it show notice?
6. Does it show response after notice?
7. Does it identify ownership?
8. Does it change the safety chronology?
9. Does it alter causation?
10. **Does it merely update a local label, or does it reconstruct the founding relationship?**

A later note does not defeat this architecture because it is later or sophisticated.

> **The case does not become clearer by volume. It becomes clearer when every record is forced to answer the same questions about pain, relationship, notice, choice, ownership, safety and consequence.**
