# 1. The audited centre

The strongest case remains narrow:

> **The urgent neurological problem contained painful abnormal feet, gait, tone, toe clawing and movement dysfunction. Neurology detected severe Parkinsonism and a dystonic-looking lower-limb gait. The question is whether the painful structural feet were permitted to proceed separately before their neurological relationship had been adequately investigated, when later evidence should have reopened that separation, and what incremental harm the delay probably caused.**

Shortest form:

> **The relationship was unresolved. The separation was permitted. What clinical work earned it?**

# 2. Essential counterfacts that must now be built into the claimant case

The case is stronger when these are conceded rather than avoided.

## 2.1 Podiatry pre-dated the neurological synthesis

Primary care had already identified genuine structural foot disease and initiated Podiatry in April 2017.

On 6 July the GP telephoned Podiatry and recorded that the case would be prioritised **“as Parkinson's.”**

Therefore:

> **Do not plead that Neurology invented the Podiatry pathway or that no neurological label reached Podiatry.**

The narrower issue is whether anyone retained the unresolved **dystonia / tone / gait / loading / pain relationship**.

## 2.2 Podiatry was not simply another GUH department

A later GUH response states that Merlin Park Podiatry was part of **Community Healthcare West** and **“is not a hospital service.”**

Therefore the systems case is cross-service:

> **primary care ↔ GUH Neurology ↔ Community Healthcare West Podiatry.**

## 2.3 Allied-health involvement existed

Physiotherapy and Occupational Therapy are listed as consulted and the neurological examination was videoed.

Therefore:

> **Do not plead “no MDT” or “nobody examined gait / feet” as an established fact.**

Ask what those services actually did and obtain the original records / video if available.

## 2.4 Later reassessment was real

The 2023 record contains OFF-dystonia reasoning, neuropathic / Fabry exploration, OFF-related leg pain / shuffling assessment and treatment change.

Therefore:

> **Do not describe the later record as clinically inert.**

The argument is whether local reassessment ever reconstructed the original integrated relationship.

## 2.5 Later dopaminergic pain response is not exclusive proof

The record supports Sinemet-related improvement in the experience of foot pain, while the clinician continued to distinguish direct Parkinsonian pain from pain modulation and recognised dyskinesia risk.

Therefore:

> **Use later response as a crossing / reopening fact, not as proof that all pain was dystonia.**

# 3. Best-case expert findings sought

## Duty

> **Reasonable Neurology was required to investigate and retain ownership of the neurological relationship between the detected lower-limb motor disorder and disabling feet sufficiently to avoid treating those feet as independent before there was an adequate basis for doing so.**

## Founding assessment breach

> **The surviving record does not demonstrate relationship-directed examination sufficient to justify the later clinical separation.**

This must remain expert-dependent because the video / allied-health record may contain additional work.

## Segregation breach

> **“May have been completely separate” gave operational direction to uncertainty before sufficient discriminating work had been demonstrated.**

## Neuropathy branch

> **Peripheral neuropathy performed explanatory work in the synthesis without the complete record preserving a formal foot sensory / reflex examination capable of clearly supporting that branch.**

Desired expert finding:

> **Reasonable practice required enough positive neurological examination evidence to support neuropathy before it could justify separation or reduce the need for integrated follow-up.**

## “Markedly divorced”

> **The patient's report that foot pain and bodily tension behaved differently established non-identity of experience, not necessarily independence of mechanism.**

Desired expert finding:

> **Further clinical work was required before that distinction could support mechanistic separation.**

## Protest

> **Once the patient expressly said the feet had not been dealt with, reasonable practice required reconsideration, explanation or a defined reintegration plan before closure was maintained.**

## Handoff / ownership

> **Podiatry was appropriate structural care, but reasonable Neurology still had to retain or create ownership of the neurological relationship if that relationship remained unresolved.**

## 2017–April 2018 reopening

> **Even if July care was reasonable, the later treatment-state crossings created a fresh clinical question. By April 2018 the treating neurologist had himself documented a focal foot phenomenon changing rapidly during the earlier procyclidine withdrawal.**

Desired finding:

> **That evidence required reconsideration of complete separation or, at minimum, a directed reassessment of the relationship.**

## Later Neurology reacquisition

> **After the last recorded Podiatry attendance, Neurology itself repeatedly possessed foot, gait, OFF, dystonia and treatment-state evidence capable of testing the original separation.**

Desired finding:

> **At an identifiable later threshold, cumulative evidence made continued non-reconstruction unreasonable.**

## Governance

Governance is not a clinical breach theory by itself.

The strongest institutional proposition is:

> **Once the historical framing issue was expressly placed before QPS / PAR / SIMT, the institution had an opportunity to review what clinical work had justified the founding separation. The reproduced SIMT material does not show a source-by-source reconstruction of that question.**

This remains an evidential / governance inquiry, not an expert-negligence conclusion.

The complete-record audit identifies twenty examination / question domains without an adequate documentary counterpart.

The useful finding is narrower:

> **The explicit documentary negatives are concentrated in dynamic dystonia / gait characterisation, peripheral sensory / reflex discrimination and directed local mechanical examination — precisely the types of work capable of distinguishing the competing explanations advanced in the record.**

Especially useful neuropathy-related negatives:

- formal light touch;
- pinprick;
- vibration;
- joint-position sense;
- Achilles reflex;

Desired expert answer:

# 9. Duty–Breach–Harm–Causation chain

## Duty

> **Investigate sufficiently before treating the neurological relationship as separate.**

## Breach

> **If expert evidence supports it: components detected; discriminating relationship work insufficiently demonstrated; separation nevertheless permitted / preserved.**

## Harm

The harm is clinical, not merely documentary:

> **loss of integrated ownership → delayed recognition / management of a modifiable or mixed component → additional pain, loading-related morbidity, mobility limitation or functional loss.**

Potential primary-care information harm is separate:

> **specialist disposition → GP reasonably understands the integrated question to have been worked through → neurological reintegration occurs later than it otherwise probably would.**

## Causation

> **competent assessment / reassessment → probable finding → probable formulation change → probable management change → probable effect on motor state / gait / loading / pain → probable reduction in incremental morbidity.**

No breach proposition should be allowed to float without a counterfactual.
