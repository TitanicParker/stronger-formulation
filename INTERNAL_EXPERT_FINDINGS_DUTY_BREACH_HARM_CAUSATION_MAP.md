# Internal Counsel Map — Best-Case Expert Findings, What Each Question Elicits, and the Duty–Breach–Harm–Causation Chain

**Status:** INTERNAL / CLAIMANT-SIDE WORKING DOCUMENT — NOT AN EXPERT INSTRUCTION  
**Purpose:** To identify, from the existing record and analytical documents, the strongest findings we would hope an independent neurological expert could responsibly make; to explain what each expert question is designed to elicit; and to show how each answer feeds **Duty (D), Breach (B), Harm (H), and Causation (C)**.

> **Important discipline:** This is a best-case claimant-side map, not a script for the expert. It must not be presented as a set of predetermined answers. Where the documents support only a question or inference, this document says so. The expert must remain free to reject, qualify or narrow any proposition.

---

# 1. The refined central case

The case is no longer best described as an “unresolved neurological–foot boundary.”

The **relationship** was unresolved.

The **boundary / segregation** was the clinical-documentary response that followed.

The refined sequence is:

> **Integrated clinical problem → unresolved neurological–foot relationship → separate documentary / operational disposition → neurological–foot segregation placed into the record → later evidence repeatedly crosses the segregation → evidence handled locally rather than used to reconstruct the original relationship → founding segregation becomes clinically and documentarily resilient.**

The strongest short formulation is:

> **The relationship was unresolved.  
> The segregation was imposed.  
> Later evidence crossed it.  
> The segregation survived.**

The key claimant-side proposition for expert testing is:

> **The founding record did not merely preserve uncertainty. It allowed the painful feet to proceed as a separate clinical problem before the relationship between the dystonic-looking lower-limb motor disorder, gait, loading, structural foot pathology and pain had been adequately determined.**

That is where **duty, breach and causation** begin to connect.

# 2. The best-case expert conclusions we are trying to establish

If the record supports them, the strongest expert conclusions would be:

## 2.1 Duty

> **A reasonably competent neurologist faced with this referral and bedside phenotype was required to investigate and retain ownership of the relationship between the abnormal lower-limb movement disorder and the disabling feet sufficiently to avoid premature segregation.**

Neurology did **not** have to own every corn, callus or podiatric lesion.

But it did have to own, or create a mechanism to resolve:

> **motor state ↔ gait ↔ loading ↔ structural lesion ↔ pain.**

## 2.2 Founding breach

> **The feet–gait–tone relationship was not investigated to a standard sufficient to justify allowing the feet to proceed as clinically separate.**

The strongest criticism is therefore **not failure to see the sign**.

The sign was seen.

It is:

> **failure adequately to integrate a detected dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon with the painful structural feet before segregation acquired operational force.**

## 2.3 Segregation breach

> **The existence of structural foot pathology did not establish neurological independence, and the record did not demonstrate sufficient clinical work to justify segregation.**

Best case:

> **The feet should have remained an active integrated neurological question, even while Podiatry treated the structural lesions.**

## 2.4 Protest breach

> **The Protest supplied express notice before closure that the central presenting problem remained unfinished; reasonable care required reassessment, explanation, specialist input or a defined reintegration plan before maintaining the segregation.**

## 2.5 Handoff breach

> **Podiatry referral was appropriate, but the handoff did not visibly carry the unresolved neurological question or a return mechanism capable of resolving it.**

Thus:

> **competent local owners existed, but the relationship had no clear owner.**

## 2.6 Independent 2017–2018 reopening breach

> **Even if July uncertainty or separation was initially reasonable, the later neurological treatment-state changes—culminating in the April 2018 clinician-authored focal foot-state observation—required the original relationship to be reopened.**

This is the strongest independent backstop because it does not depend heavily on the Protest.

## 2.7 Longitudinal breach

> **Later care repeatedly recognised facts crossing the original partition, but those facts were mainly interpreted locally—OFF, dystonia, Podiatry, pain, medication response—without equivalent reconstruction of the founding integrated problem.**

The phrase remains:

> **Local updating, global non-revision.**

## 2.8 Direct harm

If the expert supports a neurologically modifiable or mixed mechanism:

> **Premature segregation deprived the patient of integrated neurological ownership and delayed recognition / management of a component of the foot disorder that could probably have been modified, thereby prolonging pain, abnormal loading, gait limitation, recurrent mechanical morbidity or functional restriction.**

## 2.9 Primary-care information harm

> **The specialist discharge state could reasonably cause the GP to understand that Neurology had investigated the integrated question and had reached considered residual uncertainty / separateness, when the true problem may have been that the relationship had not been adequately investigated before segregation.**

Best case:

> **That specialist information state materially reduced primary care’s ability or reason to seek neurological reintegration when later evidence emerged.**

## 2.10 Causation

The strongest causal conclusion would be:

> **On the balance of clinical probability, competent integrated assessment or later reintegration at an identifiable threshold would have altered the formulation and management and materially reduced an identifiable portion of the later pain, mechanical stress, mobility restriction or functional loss.**

The expert must separately identify what would have occurred anyway because of Parkin disease, genuine structural foot pathology and treatment toxicity.

# 6. The harm architecture

The best case contains **two different harms** and they should not be confused.

## 6.1 Direct clinical harm from segregation

The claimant-side causal theory is:

> **Segregation removed the motor–foot relationship from active ownership.**

That could cause harm by delaying:

- identification of dystonic / postural / OFF-state contribution;
- treatment-state comparison;
- gait / loading modification;
- movement-disorders management;
- integrated Podiatry / neurological interpretation;
- orthotic / physiotherapy strategy targeted to neurological loading;
- medication adjustment where benefits outweighed risks.

The expert should be asked to identify the **first date** at which competent integration would probably have altered management and the **specific morbidity** that would probably have been reduced thereafter.

The strongest damages formulation is **incremental harm**, not total disease burden.

---

## 6.2 Primary-care information harm

The second pathway is different:

> **The GP sent one integrated question into specialist care and received back a high-authority specialist representation in which the neurological syndrome was resolved at high resolution while the foot relationship travelled as “Not Clear” and through Podiatry.**

Best-case expert finding:

> **A reasonable GP would understand that specialist Neurology had investigated the relationship and had reached considered residual uncertainty; the record did not expose whether the relationship had instead been insufficiently investigated before separate disposition.**

Potential consequence:

> **Later evidence that might otherwise have prompted rapid neurological return could be interpreted inside the specialist categories already supplied.**

This does not make Brennan negligent or passive.

It identifies the informational consequence of the specialist segregation.

# 8. The primary-care causation chain

This requires its own explicit expert opinion.

The desired chain is:

> **Integrated GP referral  
> → Neurology segregates unresolved feet  
> → authoritative specialist discharge state  
> → GP reasonably believes the relationship has been investigated and remains residually uncertain/separate  
> → later cross-boundary evidence is less likely to trigger immediate specialist reconstruction  
> → reintegration occurs later than it otherwise probably would  
> → additional morbidity persists during that delay.**

The questions that must be answered are:

1. What would a reasonable GP understand from the discharge communication?
2. What information necessary to recognise continuing neurological ownership was absent?
3. What later observation would probably have triggered earlier return if the uncertainty had been communicated as **unfinished work rather than considered separation**?
4. When would that return probably have occurred?
5. What would Neurology probably have done?
6. What morbidity would probably have been reduced?

Without those steps, “the record harmed primary care” remains an attractive theory rather than proved causation.
