# Meet the Team

## Expertise, decisions, information states and behaviour states — Galway Neurology and the case, 2013–2021

## Purpose

This document is no longer simply a list of who the relevant clinicians were.

The case requires a more exact map. For each actor it asks:

1. **What expertise did this person demonstrably possess?**
2. **What did this person know directly from the patient?**
3. **What did this person know only because another clinician or document told them?**
4. **What decisions did this person actually participate in?**
5. **Did the July 2017 discharge synopsis create an information state for them, or was the synopsis itself an act of behaviour and commitment by them?**
6. **When did later evidence create a genuine opportunity to reopen the inherited formulation?**

This is not a guilt map. Expertise is not responsibility, authorship is not proof of motive, and a downstream clinician is not responsible for a founding information state merely because he or she later inherited it.

The controlling distinction is:

> **The discharge synopsis did not have the same effect on every actor. For some it created an information state. For the clinician who possessed the underlying encounter and authored the operative formulation, it was a behaviour and commitment state.**

That distinction is essential to the longitudinal case.

---

# 1. The state model

## 1.1 Presentation state

The presentation state is the patient as physically and historically available before the final specialist synthesis was constructed.

For this case that state begins with the GP chronology and culminates in the July 2017 neurological admission. The originating object was not “Parkinsonism plus unrelated feet.” It was an integrated problem involving:

- painful and abnormal feet;
- abnormal gait;
- difficulty initiating movement;
- increased tone and stiffness;
- inability to relax;
- clawing/postural abnormality;
- impaired dexterity;
- deteriorating function;
- emerging Parkinsonian signs.

The GP referral is therefore not merely background correspondence. It defines the clinical question that entered Neurology.

## 1.2 Information state

An information state exists when an actor who did not possess the underlying encounter has to understand the patient through the account transmitted to them.

A discharge synopsis can therefore do more than report facts. It can determine:

- which problems appear integrated;
- which problems appear separate;
- which questions appear already investigated;
- which uncertainties appear to be genuine residual medical uncertainty;
- and which facts disappear from the portable representation altogether.

The patient, the GP and the movement-disorders nurse can all be placed into downstream information states by a specialist discharge synthesis, although each begins with a different amount of independent knowledge.

## 1.3 Behaviour / commitment state

The authoring neurologist is different.

He is not learning the admission from his own discharge summary. He already possesses the underlying encounter.

For him, the act of compiling, confirming and distributing the discharge synthesis is therefore not principally an information event. It is a **behavioural and commitment event**.

He moves from:

> **knowing the patient in the encounter**

into:

> **choosing the representation of the patient that will leave the encounter and become authoritative downstream.**

That distinction must be maintained throughout the case.

## 1.4 Reopening state

A downstream actor may later acquire new first-hand information — treatment response, withdrawal effects, examination findings, OFF-state phenomena, foot dystonia, pain response, gait change, or other observations — that can no longer be attributed merely to inheritance of the July formulation.

At that point a new question arises:

> **Did the new information require the inherited frame to be reopened?**

Responsibility therefore follows **information opportunity**, not job title alone.

# 2. The Protest is the phase change

The Protest is treated in this project as a fact of the chronology.

Before the Protest, an incompletely resolved foot–neurology relationship remains part of an assessment still capable of correction while the patient is physically available.

At the Protest, the patient expressly states in substance:

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

After the Protest, the status of closure changes.

The final synthesis is no longer produced in an environment where the treating service can be assumed simply not to know that the patient regards the original problem as unfinished. It is produced after express notice.

That is why **“Not Clear”** matters.

“Not Clear” is not the voice of the GP. It is not the voice of the patient. It is the post-Protest voice of the department dealing in its final synthesis with the residual foot question that entered Neurology through the GP referral.

The question is not whether uncertainty was medically permissible.

The question is:

> **What clinical work earned that uncertainty?**

# 4. Dr Aaron Brennan

## General Practitioner — input comparator and downstream information-state recipient

Galway East Medical Practice records that Aaron Brennan graduated in medicine from NUI Galway in 1998 and worked in hospital medicine before specialist GP training, including experience in Neurology.

Public source:
- https://www.galwayeastmedicalpractice.ie/team/

Brennan's forensic importance does not depend on him being a movement-disorders specialist.

He is important because he knows the patient **before the specialist documentary patient exists**.

## 4.1 What Brennan sends into Neurology

Across April to late June 2017 the GP chronology progressively joins:

- bilateral foot pain and abnormal feet;
- abnormal/festinant or stiff gait;
- difficulty initiating movement;
- muscle tension and inability to relax;
- increased tone;
- toe clawing;
- impaired dexterity;
- deterioration at work and in ordinary function.

By 29 June this has become an integrated neurological referral question.

The correct starting proposition is therefore:

> **Brennan sent an integrated feet–gait–tone–movement patient into Neurology.**

That referral defines the standard against which later improvement can also be observed. When procyclidine later produces marked functional improvement, Brennan is not seeing a random improvement in an abstract Parkinson diagnosis. He can compare the patient against the integrated problem he personally referred.

## 4.2 What the discharge synthesis does to Brennan's information state

Brennan does not witness the eight days of neurological bedside work.

When the specialist discharge account arrives, he is entitled in ordinary clinical communication to treat it as the result of the specialist admission he requested.

That means **“Not Clear”** does not arrive to him as a naked phrase.

It arrives carrying the apparent authority of:

- an eight-day neurological admission;
- a consultant neurologist;
- sophisticated young-onset Parkinsonian reasoning;
- recessive / PARK2 reasoning;
- departmental and colleague input;
- a DAT scan;
- a treatment plan;
- and a completed specialist discharge product.

The documentary effect is therefore significant.

Brennan cannot tell from the words “Not Clear” alone whether the true underlying state was:

> **A. the relationship was adequately investigated and remained medically unclear**

or:

> **B. the relationship remained unclear because the clinical work necessary to resolve it was not performed or not completed.**

Those are radically different states.

If the surviving evidence ultimately establishes B, then the documentary problem is exact:

> **an insufficiency of examination has been represented downstream as an insufficiency of medical knowledge.**

That is why the phrase is not an innocent fact from the GP's point of view.

It can create a specialist information state in which the GP reasonably understands his referral question to have been researched and answered as far as Neurology was able to answer it.

## 4.3 Brennan retains one protection against the documentary state

Brennan is not identical to Browne because Brennan retains his own pre-synthesis memory.

He knows what he sent in.

That makes him a powerful longitudinal comparator when later treatment response restores function according to the original referral standard.

But the specialist synthesis still changes the interpretive environment in which he receives those later observations.

# 5. Dr Thomas Monaghan

## Consultant Neurologist — authorial centre, behaviour state and commitment state

Public consultant material identifies Thomas Monaghan as a Consultant Neurologist practising through University Hospital Galway.

Public source:
- https://www.vhi.ie/members/directory-of-consultants/consultant-details/12360

His publicly visible academic footprint is broader general neurology than the concentrated Parkin/dystonia publication trail of Counihan, Browne and Healy. That distinction should remain explicit.

His importance in this case is patient-specific.

## 5.1 Monaghan possesses the founding information advantage

Monaghan is differently situated from every downstream recipient.

He has access to:

- the GP referral;
- the patient himself;
- the bedside presentation;
- the documented abnormal gait and tone;
- the dystonic-looking lower-limb posture;
- the foot pain history;
- the course of the admission;
- and the Protest.

He also occupies the authorial position from which the final discharge synthesis is created.

He is therefore the actor best placed to know the difference between:

> **what was actually established at the bedside**

and:

> **what the final documentary patient says.**

## 5.2 The discharge synopsis is not an information state for Monaghan

This distinction must not be blurred later.

Monaghan cannot be analysed as though he subsequently “worked from” the discharge summary in the same way as Brennan or Browne.

The summary does not tell him what happened.

He is participating in the act that tells **other people** what happened.

For him the synopsis therefore constitutes a **behaviour / commitment state**.

The central question becomes:

> **What consequences follow once the treating neurologist has chosen this formulation as the authoritative account of the encounter?**

Those consequences can include:

- how the original referral question is represented;
- how the Protest is or is not transmitted;
- what is treated as already settled;
- which uncertainty is exported;
- what later evidence is required before the formulation is reopened;
- and how later clinicians understand the patient's feet in relation to Parkinsonism.

## 5.3 The acceleration problem

The surviving bedside material documents a real and striking Parkinsonian syndrome and a dystonic-looking lower-limb presentation.

The final synthesis then accelerates into substantially more sophisticated neurological and neurogenetic reasoning, including recessive/PARK2 reasoning, colleague/departmental context, genetic counselling and treatment strategy.

That creates the controlling audit proposition:

> **The synthesis may have outrun the examination.**

For Monaghan, that is not merely an epistemic question. It is also an authorship question:

> **What work, and whose work, earned the sophistication of the final formulation?**

And for the feet specifically:

> **What work earned the post-Protest conclusion “Not Clear”?**

## 5.4 “Not Clear” as behavioural commitment

Downstream, “Not Clear” creates information.

For Monaghan, it records a decision about how the unresolved relationship will be represented at closure.

That is the distinction the case must preserve:

> **The summary changes knowledge downstream; it changes commitment upstream.**

# 6. Dr/Prof Timothy J. Counihan

## Consultant Neurologist — internal specialist capability and a patient-specific treatment decision

Counihan is important because his public expertise connects directly with the conceptual territory of this case:

- young-onset Parkinsonism;
- Parkin/PARK2/PRKN genetics;
- dystonia;
- abnormal gait;
- freezing of gait;
- movement-disorders practice.

But his importance is no longer confined to capability.

He also enters the patient-specific decision chain.

## 6.1 Anchor: JAMA Neurology 2013 — Parkin disease

**Doherty KM, Silveira-Moriyama L, Parkkinen L, et al. _Parkin Disease: A Clinicopathologic Entity?_ JAMA Neurology. 2013;70(5):571–579. DOI: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2013.172.**

Relevant authors in this case network include:

- **Daniel G. Healy**
- **Timothy J. Counihan**

The paper concerns parkin/PARK2 disease, an autosomal-recessive juvenile/young-onset parkinsonism. The reported presenting signs included tremor often combined with dystonia; later features included freezing of gait and postural deformity. Counihan contributed acquisition of data, and Healy participated in study design and analysis/interpretation.

Sources:
- https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/1659731
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23459986/

This establishes a real pre-2017 professional and research connection between Healy and Counihan in the disease family that later became central to this patient's case.

## 6.2 Anchor: 2015 — _Genetics in Parkinson's disease – what have we learned?_

The Galway article published on 1 June 2015 was authored by:

- **Patrick Browne — Movement Disorders ANP Candidate, Neurology Department, Galway University Hospital**
- **Timothy Counihan — Consultant Neurologist, Neurology Department, Galway University Hospital**
- Patrick Cullinane
- Aoife Corcoran

The article expressly discusses Parkinson genetics including **Parkin (PRKN)**.

Source:
- https://www.irishhealthpro.com/content/articles/print/name/genetics-in-parkinsons-disease--what-have-we-learned

The patient-specific record identifies the Galway departmental Parkinson-genetics material as part of the discharge packet accompanying the specialist correspondence. That is important for two reasons.

First, it shows that departmental genetic expertise did not merely exist somewhere in the background. It was selected as relevant enough to this patient to travel with the discharge material.

Second, it produces a striking asymmetry in the same package:

> **high-resolution departmental genetics reasoning travelled outward, while the relationship of the painful feet to the neurological disorder travelled outward as “Not Clear.”**

The question is not whether that asymmetry proves breach. It asks what clinical work justified it.

## 6.3 Patient-specific decision: levodopa deferral

The later correspondence contains Monaghan's statement:

> **“Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?”**

The contemporaneous line itself identifies “Tim”; later clinician material identifies that Tim as **Timothy Counihan**.

This means Counihan is not merely an academic comparator standing outside the case.

He is part of the internal specialist decision environment concerning treatment strategy.

The evidential discipline remains important:

- do not infer from this alone that Counihan performed the bedside foot examination;
- do not assume precisely when his patient-specific involvement began;
- do not let later identification of his role travel backwards beyond what the source supports.

But once his participation in treatment deferral is established, the correct question becomes:

> **What information about the unresolved feet was available to the specialist decision network when that treatment strategy was being chosen?**

# 7. Patrick Browne

## Movement Disorders ANP / Parkinson nurse — relevant expert in a downstream information state

Patrick Browne is the Galway movement-disorders nurse/ANP relevant to this case.

His public record is unusually close to the disputed clinical territory.

The 2015 genetics article identifies him as **Movement Disorders ANP Candidate, Neurology Department, Galway University Hospital** and co-authors him with Counihan on Parkinson genetics and PRKN.

The 2017 Neurology paper places him with Counihan in a Galway clinical-reasoning case involving dystonic foot phenomenology and parkin-related juvenile Parkinsonism.

## 7.1 Anchor: Neurology 2017 — _A demure teenager and her dystonic foot_

**Cullinane PW, Browne P, Hennessy MJ, Counihan TJ. _Clinical Reasoning: A demure teenager and her dystonic foot._ Neurology. 2017;89(7):e71–e75. DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000004231.**

The case begins with a 13-year-old girl and a four-year history of abnormal gait. The apparent foot-drop-type presentation is phenomenologically classified as dystonia; further examination reveals Parkinsonian features, and genetic testing identifies parkin mutations producing autosomal-recessive juvenile parkinsonism. Treatment includes procyclidine.

Sources:
- https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000004231
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28808173/

The conceptual cluster is exceptionally close:

> **young patient → abnormal gait → foot posture → dystonia → Parkinsonian signs → parkin genetics → procyclidine**

The paper was published online on 14 August 2017, shortly after this patient's admission. It must not be projected backwards as proof that its reasoning was applied to this patient in July. Its proper use is to establish the actual movement-disorders capability present in the Galway environment.

## 7.2 Remove the holiday theory

This document does **not** rely on the proposition that Browne was on holiday.

The relevant point is evidentially narrower and stronger.

The July synthesis itself records:

> **Parkinsons Disease Advanced Nurse Practitioner input — pending.**

It later contemplates the Parkinson nurse bringing the patient in for a further discussion.

The present record therefore does not place the movement-disorders ANP inside the founding bedside encounter that generated the discharge synthesis.

That is the fact that matters.

## 7.3 Browne's founding state is informational, not behavioural

Browne's expertise may be exactly the expertise one would wish to have available when confronting a young Parkinsonian patient with dystonic-looking foot/lower-limb phenomena.

But expertise does not create retrospective access to an encounter he did not witness.

If his involvement begins after the discharge synthesis has already been created, then his initial patient is necessarily mediated by:

- the discharge synopsis;
- the consultant formulation;
- the handover information;
- and whatever the patient later tells or demonstrates to him directly.

He therefore begins in a **downstream information state**.

He cannot know from personal observation:

- the full quality of the original gait;
- the complete dynamic foot posture during admission;
- what examination was actually performed on the painful feet;
- what examination was not performed;
- the degree to which the final synthesis exceeded the documented bedside work;
- or the Protest, unless it was transmitted to him.

That means Browne must not be assigned responsibility for the founding state merely because his expertise was highly relevant.

The correct rule is:

> **Browne's potential responsibility begins only when he acquires enough independent information to test or reopen the inherited frame.**

This is why his later medication and movement-disorders role must be analysed separately from July 2017.

The patient's account identifies Browne as the movement-disorders nurse contacted when medication problems arose and as the clinician involved in later Sinemet prescribing. Those later encounters matter because they may create new direct-information states. Each should be tied to its own dated primary record before being used as a breach proposition.

## 7.4 Browne as the clean output comparator

Browne is therefore exceptionally useful not because he expands founding responsibility but because he limits it.

Brennan knows the patient before the synthesis.

Monaghan knows the patient during the encounter and creates the synthesis.

Browne has highly relevant movement-disorders expertise but, on the present record, does not possess the founding bedside presentation independently of the account transmitted after it.

The shortest formulation is:

> **Patrick Browne could not inherit the bedside examination. He could inherit only what the department made portable, until later direct encounters gave him something new.**

# 11. The crucial asymmetry: expertise entered the case unevenly

The significance of the expertise map is not simply that Galway knew about Parkin disease.

The patient-specific material shows sophisticated expertise entering the case in some directions:

- PARK2 / recessive genetics;
- genetic counselling;
- specialist referral to Healy;
- a departmental Parkinson-genetics article supplied with the discharge material;
- a treatment strategy involving Counihan and the decision to defer levodopa.

At the same time, the original painful-foot relationship reaches closure as:

> **“Not Clear.”**

That creates the central asymmetry:

> **The genetic and treatment synthesis became highly resolved while the originating feet–neurology relationship remained unresolved.**

The forensic question is not why every uncertainty was not eliminated.

It is why sophistication travelled so far in one direction without a visible equivalent depth of clinical work in the other.

# 13. Behavioural consequences of commitment

Once the July synthesis is made authoritative, later behaviour can be influenced by what has already been committed to record.

That does not require motive, concealment or bad faith.

A professional formulation can become self-stabilising simply because:

- later referrals begin from it;
- later clinicians inherit it;
- new evidence is interpreted locally rather than globally;
- apparently settled boundaries require a stronger trigger to reopen;
- and the original unformulated patient is no longer physically available in the same untreated state.

This is why the discharge synopsis is not merely a historical document.

It is an intervention in the future information environment.

For downstream actors it says what the patient is.

For its author it records what has been committed.

The case must examine both effects.
