# Meet the Team

**Purpose:** Show who knew what, when they knew it, and what question each person or service was in a position to answer.

This is not a blame map.

The rule is simple:

> **Responsibility follows information and opportunity.**

---

# 1. Aaron Brennan — the GP who sent the whole problem in

Brennan knew the patient before Neurology.

He sent in a problem involving:

- painful feet;
- abnormal gait;
- increased tone / stiffness;
- movement difficulty;
- toe clawing;
- declining function.

He later received a specialist account in which the neurological disorder was strongly described, while the foot pain could still be separate / neuropathic / “Not clear.”

The simple question is:

> **What would a reasonable GP understand had already been examined and decided?**

In March 2019 Brennan again linked the feet to increased tone, abnormal gait and Parkinson's disease.

That makes him important both at the beginning and later when the problem was being joined back together.

---

# 2. Thomas Monaghan — the founding neurologist

Monaghan had the patient, the referral, the abnormal gait, the painful feet, the neurological findings and the later specialist synthesis.

The central questions for him are simple:

1. **What examination showed whether the neurological problem was contributing to the painful feet?**
2. **What supported peripheral neuropathy?**
3. **What justified saying the foot problem might be completely separate?**
4. **What should have changed when the patient said his feet had not been dealt with?**
5. **What should have changed when neurological medication later altered the foot problem?**

No allegation about motive is needed.

---

# 3. Timothy Counihan — relevant specialist expertise

Counihan had relevant movement-disorders / young-onset Parkinsonism expertise.

The record supports patient-specific treatment discussion, but not a documented bedside foot examination by him.

The question is:

> **Did his expertise actually reach the unresolved foot problem, or only the wider Parkinsonism / treatment theory?**

---

# 4. Daniel Healy — later independent specialist opinion

Healy reviewed the patient later in 2017 and thought he probably had PARK2 / Parkin-type disease.

That was important later evidence.

But it does not automatically answer the foot question.

The simple question is:

> **Was Healy ever asked to explain the disabling foot pain or its relationship to the abnormal gait?**

If not, his authority on Parkin does not settle that separate question.

---

# 5. Patrick Browne — later Movement Disorders information

Browne was involved later in treatment and symptom management.

By then he was receiving a patient whose case had already been framed for years.

The question is not whether he inherited the old record.

It is:

> **When did he acquire enough new information of his own that the original foot question should have been asked again?**

Later OFF-related pain, shuffling and treatment response are important here.

---

# 6. Podiatry — the structural-foot service

Podiatry documented genuine local problems:

- recurrent callus / corn;
- clawed toes;
- pressure points;
- calf tightness;
- offloading and insoles.

Podiatry was appropriate.

The issue is not whether Podiatry should have treated those problems.

The issue is:

> **Who was responsible for asking why those pressure and structural problems existed in a patient with a very abnormal neurological gait?**

And:

> **Who was responsible for bringing Podiatry's findings back together with Neurology's findings?**

The later record says this Podiatry service belonged to Community Healthcare West, not GUH itself.

---

# 7. Galway Neurology after 2019 — the feet come back inside Neurology

After the last recorded Podiatry attendance, Neurology itself later records:

- Parkinsonian-looking foot movement;
- foot sensitivity and excess movement;
- morning foot problems;
- severe pain;
- possible OFF dystonia;
- OFF-related leg pain and shuffling;
- toe curling;
- pain benefit from Sinemet.

The simple question is:

> **Once Neurology itself had the feet back in front of it, when was the original source-of-pain question rebuilt?**

---

# 8. QPS / PAR / SIMT — the governance stage

By 2025 the patient expressly asked the hospital to review the long-running diagnostic framing.

The matter went through QPS, PAR and SIMT.

The outcome said there was **“no deficit in care.”**

The record we have does not show the full PAR evidence pack or a source-by-source reconstruction of the 2017 foot question.

The institutional question is:

> **Did governance go back to the beginning and ask what examination had justified treating the painful feet as separate?**

That is different from simply asking whether the completed record looked reasonable.

---

# 9. St Vincent's — later independent information

The February 2026 genetic letter confirms two pathogenic Parkin variants and states that the patient's Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound-heterozygous Parkin mutations.

That is important later information.

It does not rewrite 2017.

The question remains what earlier clinicians should have done with the information they actually had at the time.

---

# 10. The actor map in one line each

- **Brennan:** sent the whole problem in.
