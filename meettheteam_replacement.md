# Meet the Team

## Expertise, decisions, information states and behaviour states

**Status:** RECORD-WIDE AUDIT VERSION

**Function:** Identify who possessed which information, who participated in which decisions, and how the unresolved feet–movement relationship changed custody across primary care, GUH Neurology, Community Healthcare West Podiatry and later governance.

It is not a guilt map.

The controlling distinctions are:

> **Capability is not participation, and participation is not bedside application.**

> **Clinical inheritance is not the same thing as allocation authorship.**

> **Clinical inheritance is not the same thing as governance adoption.**

> **Responsibility follows information opportunity.**

---

# 1. The pre-Neurology state matters

The patient did not reach Neurology with no foot pathway.

Primary care had already documented bilateral foot pain, mallet toes, callus / pressure areas and abnormal gait and had initiated Podiatry.

On 6 July 2017, while the neurological admission was ongoing, Brennan recorded:

> **“Phoned podiatry to discuss case - will mark as priority case and as Parkinson's - should be called mid September.”**

This is a crucial counterfact.

It means:

- Podiatry was already a legitimate structural-foot pathway;
- Parkinsonism was communicated to that service;
- the case is **not** that Neurology invented Podiatry or that Podiatry was inherently inappropriate.

The actor question is narrower:

> **Who retained responsibility for the unresolved neurological relationship between motor state, gait, loading, structural pathology and pain?**

---

# 2. The service boundary was broader than “two hospital departments”

A later GUH data-access response states that the Merlin Park Podiatry service was part of **Community Healthcare West** and **“is not a hospital service.”**

The relevant system is therefore:

> **primary care ↔ GUH Neurology ↔ Community Healthcare West Podiatry**

rather than a simple internal GUH Neurology/Podiatry split.

That makes explicit communication content and return mechanisms more important.

> **Being marked “as Parkinson's” to Podiatry is not the same as transmitting a defined dystonia–gait–loading question.**

# 3. The state model

## Presentation state

The urgent neurological referral contains bilateral foot problems, abnormal gait, pathological tone / stiffness, movement-initiation difficulty, toe clawing and functional deterioration.

It does not establish neurological independence of the feet.

## Bedside-detection state

Neurology detects severe young-onset Parkinsonism, a strikingly abnormal gait, a foot-drop-like phenomenon considered dystonic rather than true foot drop, pes planus and hammertoes.

The examination is videoed. Physiotherapy and Occupational Therapy are listed as consulted.

Therefore do not say the lower limb was ignored or that there was no multidisciplinary involvement.

The narrower question is:

> **What relationship-directed foot / gait work is actually preserved, including in any video or allied-health records?**

## Specialist-synthesis state

The final synthesis says the leg / foot symptoms including corns, callouses and burning:

> **“may have been completely separate”**

or perhaps represented peripheral neuropathy, concludes:

> **“Not clear”**

and characterises the patient as having:

> **“in his own mind - markedly divorced”**

bodily / leg tension from the corns, callouses and foot pains.

## Reopening state

Later actors acquire independent information capable of testing that separation. Their responsibility depends on what they personally knew and when.

## Governance state

Once the historical framing issue is expressly put before QPS / PAR / SIMT, institutional functions are no longer merely passive recipients of a clinical record. They are reviewing an institutional account.

# 4. The explicit examination-gap test

A conservative complete-record audit identifies twenty examination / question domains for which no adequate documentary counterpart has been found.

This is **not** proof that all twenty were required or that they were not performed undocumented.

At actor level, the point is:

> **If a clinician advances complete separation or peripheral neuropathy as explanatory possibilities, what examination evidence actually supports those alternatives?**

The absence of formal documented foot light touch, pinprick, vibration, joint-position testing, Achilles reflex, plantar response and true ankle-clonus testing is particularly relevant to the neuropathy branch.

Do not call later-documented dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion or eversion absent from the complete record; Neurology expressly records those in 2020.

# 5. Aaron Brennan — input comparator, Podiatry facilitator and later re-integrator

Brennan is not merely the GP who sent the patient to Neurology.

He occupies three distinct information states.

## Before Neurology

He documents the structural feet and abnormal gait and initiates Podiatry.

## During the admission

On 6 July he telephones Podiatry and records that the case will be prioritised **“as Parkinson's.”**

This means Brennan did not himself treat the foot problem as wholly disconnected from the emerging neurological state.

## After specialist review

He receives a highly resolved neurological formulation plus possible complete separation / neuropathy / “Not clear.”

He did not witness the inpatient examination and cannot necessarily distinguish:

> **adequate specialist investigation followed by residual uncertainty**

from

> **insufficiently demonstrated investigation followed by separate disposition.**

## March 2019

Brennan again expressly links:

- feet;
- increased tone;
- abnormal gait;
- Parkinson's disease;
- clawing;
- pain.

The 2019 Podiatry referral is therefore a strong factual re-integration event.

The Brennan question is:

> **What did the specialist output cause primary care reasonably to believe had already been examined, and what did Brennan's later re-linking make available to the receiving services?**

# 15. Whole-system custody map

The record is best understood through changing custody of the relationship:

## Primary care input

Structural feet + gait become an urgent integrated neurological referral while Podiatry is already active as a separate structural-care route.

## GUH founding neurological state

Neurology identifies the movement disorder and a dystonic-looking lower-limb sign, but the final synthesis permits complete separation / neuropathy while the relationship remains “Not clear.”

## Cross-service 2017–March 2019 state

GUH Neurology and Community Healthcare West Podiatry accumulate different kinds of information, with primary care between them.

## Post-March-2019 state

No later active Podiatry treatment is shown; Neurology increasingly contains the foot evidence itself.

## 2025–26 governance state

The historical issue becomes an express institutional review object.

The controlling question is:

> **At each threshold, who actually possessed enough information to test the relationship, and what did that information make reasonable care or governance capable of doing?**
