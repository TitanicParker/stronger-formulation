The proposition

LEAVE IT AT THAT

A four-part reconstruction of one surviving choice — with a short satirical coda

Working manuscript — v0.1
Form: four linked instalments, each intended to become a 15-minute / c.2,500-word read, followed by a five-minute satirical coda
Centre of gravity: the post-Protest choice not to reopen the unresolved feet–gait–dystonia question
Method: literary journalism anchored to the contemporaneous record, with explicit separation between documented fact, patient recollection, inference and later interpretation

The proposition

This is not a document about one missed corn.

It is not, at root, a document about one wrong diagnosis, one drug, one consultant, one complaint or one hospital letter.

It is about a moment in which an unusually rich human problem remained open — and then ceased to be treated as open.

Near the end of an eight-day neurological admission in July 2017, after a university hospital had already recognised severe young-onset Parkinsonism, a strikingly abnormal gait, a lower limb with a foot-drop appearance suggestive of dystonic posture, marked rigidity and bradykinesia, pes planus, hammertoes and perhaps thirteen years of prior evolution, the patient objected that the feet had not been dealt with.

That objection matters because it means the omission had become visible before discharge.

The admission had not yet become history. The patient had not yet gone home. The discharge synthesis had not yet become the portable truth by which later clinicians would know him. The department still had access to the body, the gait, the feet, the soles, the pain, the story, the untreated baseline, the strange resilience, the patient's intelligence, the departmental expertise and the possibility of going back to look again.

The moment did not require clairvoyance. Nobody needed to know the final mechanism. Nobody needed to prove that every corn, callus, burning sensation or later pain state was neurological. Nobody needed to predict what procyclidine withdrawal, Podiatry, levodopa, OFF states, toe curling or the next eight years would eventually reveal.

The choice was smaller than that.

The choice was whether uncertainty would remain active.

Would the response be: we do not yet understand this relationship, so we must reopen it?

Or would it be: we do not yet understand this relationship; leave it at that?

Everything in this four-part work turns around that fork.

The first instalment asks what exactly was standing in front of the hospital before the choice was made.

The second slows time almost to a stop around the Protest and asks what remained possible in that room.

The third follows what happened when an unresolved phenotype was converted into a stable information state and then distributed across services.

The fourth returns years later, when pain had become psychologically dangerous, and asks what it means for an institution to look back at the accumulated notes and stand over a pathway whose central unresolved question had never been globally reconstructed.

The refrain is simple:

Leave it at that.

At first it is only a bedside choice.

Then it becomes a document.

Then an information architecture.

Then a management pattern.

Then a life.

Then, finally, a retrospective position.

Evidence discipline

This manuscript should be fierce in language and conservative in proof.

Use the following tags in draft until every proposition is nailed down:

  • [DOC] directly documented contemporaneously.
  • [PATIENT] patient recollection or autobiographical account.
  • [DEPT] evidence of departmental / colleague / senior involvement.
  • [EXPERTISE] evidence of relevant local academic or subspecialist capability.
  • [RX] treatment-state evidence.
  • [POD] Podiatry evidence.
  • [GP] primary-care evidence.
  • [LIFE] lived-function / autobiographical material.
  • [SAFEGUARD] evidence relevant to psychological safety, risk recognition, crisis response or its apparent absence.
  • [2025] complaint / rebuttal / governance material.
  • [INFERENCE] a reasoned interpretation that must not be written as established motive or fact.
  • [OPEN] proposition requiring verification.

The literary force should come from adjacency: place documented facts beside one another until the reader feels the discrepancy. Do not manufacture heat where chronology already burns.

INSTALLMENT I THE DINOSAUR WALKS IN What exactly did Galway have? +

What exactly did Galway have?

The story should begin before Parkinson's disease has a name.

Begin with work.

Begin with a man standing behind a bar for hours, learning the geography of pain through the soles of his feet. Begin with the physical tricks by which a body keeps a life moving long after automatic movement has begun to fail. Begin with corn plasters brought by a friend. Begin with podiatry. Begin with the strange relief of having something mechanical altered in a foot that keeps producing trouble. Begin with job changes, with walking, with the conscious management of a body that other people still read as functioning. Begin with a bicycle and Countdown on the television: ten or eleven kilometres while the body grows stranger. Begin with the fact that the person survives his own phenotype so effectively that survival itself becomes camouflage.

The eventual medical record preserves some of this. It records a possible thirteen-year onset, bodily tension, corns and calluses, fallen arches, treatment of the feet since around 2009–2010, bar work, improvement in foot pain after leaving that work, continuing leg tension, gym exercise and cycling, later difficulty in a medical-devices job and progressive loss of dexterity. It records a patient who had kept constructing solutions long before Neurology met him. [DOC]

But the record is not the life.

The life is what happened between the entries.

The important journalistic task is to recover the cost hidden inside apparent function.

He could walk. That does not mean walking was normal.

He could work. That does not mean work was physiologically cheap.

He could exercise. That does not mean exercise disproved severe disease.

He could articulate what was happening. That does not mean the system had understood it.

The line from the autobiographical work belongs early and should recur:

I was not well because I could walk. I could walk at cost.

That one sentence changes the interpretation of the entire prodrome. Resilience is no longer evidence against severity. It becomes part of the phenotype. A highly adaptive person can hold a deteriorating system together for years by spending effort other people never see.

This matters medically. It matters pedagogically. It matters morally. It matters because the patient who eventually arrives in July 2017 is not a blank 39-year-old with new Parkinsonism. He is a human longitudinal study who has already conducted thirteen years of uncontrolled natural experiments on himself: prolonged standing, walking, reduced standing, job changes, exercise, conscious gait, podiatry, rest, alcohol used as a crude de-tensioning strategy, and relentless adaptation.

He arrives carrying data.

Not data in a spreadsheet.

Data in tissue, posture, habit, deformity, skin, pain, gait and memory.

The GP sends the whole animal

The primary-care chronology is crucial because it shows what the hospital received before specialist compression began.

On 10 April 2017, the GP record pairs abnormal gait and bilateral foot pain with marked mallet toes, onychogryphosis and callus/pressure areas. The patient works on his feet and is finding it tough. [GP]

On 8 June, the GP is considering a neurology opinion specifically for the patient's gait, feet and “muscle tension” issues. [GP]

By 23 June, there is marked increased tone in both legs and brisk upper-limb reflexes. [GP]

By 29 June, the picture has accelerated into an urgent neurological problem: an unusual stiff/festinant gait, difficulty initiating movement, increased tone, clawed toes, deterioration and a question of Parkinsonism or another neurological process. [GP — insert exact referral]

The point is not that the GP had solved the mechanism.

The point is that the referral had not yet split the human being into neat domains.

Feet. Gait. Tone. Movement initiation. Deterioration.

One body.

One problem still open enough to be thought about.

The whole animal reached the hospital before the hospital divided it.

That should be one of the load-bearing sentences of Part I.

Monday, 3 July 2017

Now the doors of the university hospital open.

The patient is 39.

Neurology describes the gait as strikingly abnormal. The left lower limb has a foot-drop appearance that is considered somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture rather than true foot drop. There is no arm swing. Tone is markedly symmetrically increased. Gross rigidity is evident during walking. Gross bradykinesia is present. Pes planus and hammertoes are noted. The examination is videoed with permission. [DOC]

The eventual DAT scan request will describe the walk as “almost dystonically unusual” in a very young man with bilateral Parkinsonism and symptoms perhaps reaching back thirteen years, with an autosomal-recessive form suspected. [DOC]

Pause there.

This is the moment to let the reader see the patient before diagnostics flatten him.

A young man with an old body history.

A Parkinsonian syndrome severe enough to be obvious but atypical enough to provoke broad investigation.

Very little tremor.

Huge tone.

A gait strange enough to be called dystonic-looking.

Painful abnormal feet with years of history.

Corns and calluses.

Fallen arches.

Hammertoes.

A man whose cognition is later described as excellent, whose intelligence impresses the clinician, who understands complex counselling and expresses willingness to hear about clinical trials. [DOC]

This is not merely “young Parkinson's disease.”

It is a case with density.

Where the abnormal limb meets the ground

The soles deserve their own scene.

The soles are not a rhetorical flourish. They are the patient's load-bearing archive.

If the gait is abnormal for years, the foot is where that abnormal motor pattern repeatedly collides with gravity, footwear and the floor. The plantar surface may preserve evidence of where pressure has travelled, where compensation has become habitual, where tissue has thickened, where recurrent focal stress has accumulated.

That does not prove neurological causation of every structural lesion. Structural pathology can be genuinely structural. Mechanical pathology can coexist with dystonia. Pain can be mixed. The point is not to force a monocausal story.

The point is that the interface demanded curiosity.

In a patient with painful feet and a dystonic-looking lower-limb gait, the soles are not beneath Neurology's dignity. They are where the neurological motor programme meets the world.

The working evidential formulation must therefore be exact:

No adequate plantar / sole examination has yet been identified in the available documentation. [OPEN — verify across nursing, physio, OT, Podiatry and handwritten notes]

Do not write “nobody looked at the soles” until the record can support it.

But if the completed record contains no serious plantar examination, the absence itself is astonishing. Not because every person with Parkinsonism needs a foot-pressure laboratory, but because this patient had arrived with a long, painful foot history and an explicitly dystonic-looking gait.

The question becomes almost embarrassingly elementary:

What was written on the surface carrying thirteen years of abnormal movement?

It walked into an archaeology department

Now widen the lens from bedside to institution.

The significance of the case is not merely that relevant expertise existed somewhere in the world. Relevant expertise existed around Galway.

This section must distinguish institutional capability from personal involvement. It must never pretend that publication authors personally examined the patient unless the record proves that they did.

But capability matters.

Timothy Counihan had co-authored the 2013 JAMA Neurology paper on Parkin disease, in which parkin-linked young-onset disease and dystonia were part of the clinical landscape. [EXPERTISE — public source]

Patrick Browne and Counihan were publishing on dystonia and on Parkinsonian gait / freezing. [EXPERTISE — insert exact papers]

Galway clinicians and engineering / human-movement collaborators were already working on objective measurement of gait and freezing. [EXPERTISE]

And in August 2017, within weeks of this admission, clinicians from the Department of Neurology at University Hospital Galway published a Neurology Clinical Reasoning case built around a teenager with a dystonic foot, neurological gait disorder and parkin-related disease. [EXPERTISE — publication date 15 Aug 2017; explicitly note that publication after admission does not prove July knowledge of that particular manuscript]

The correct literary line is therefore not: “the experts definitely knew what this was.”

That would overstate.

The line is:

The dinosaur did not walk into a department unfamiliar with dinosaurs. It walked into an archaeology department.

The opportunity was not merely diagnostic.

It was educational.

Scientific.

Longitudinal.

Human.

A local patient, able to return, with an apparently long untreated or undertreated phenotype, excellent cognition, a bizarre gait, painful feet and a future full of therapeutic perturbations.

What movement-disorders academic would not want the baseline?

Shoes off.

Socks off.

Soles.

Standing.

Walking.

Turning.

Faster walking.

Fatigue.

Toe posture.

Pressure sites.

Fixed versus dynamic deformity.

Video.

Podiatry integrated with Neurology.

Then repeat the examination after treatment.

The extraordinary missed opportunity is that the body would later provide the experiment anyway.

The department studies almost everything except the relationship

The admission is not medically empty. Quite the opposite.

This is what makes the case interesting.

There is CT. MRI brain. MRI cervical spine. abdominal ultrasound to explore a Gaucher-related possibility. Extensive blood work. DAT imaging. Genetic thinking. Recessive Parkinsonism. PRKN/PARK2. Discussion of LRRK2, SNCA, GBA and future exome or genome analysis. A Neurology Complex Case Meeting. Senior discussion around levodopa and dopamine agonists. Physiotherapy. Occupational therapy. Counselling. A plan for rasagiline and procyclidine. [DOC]

This is a service thinking hard.

The DisSynth even records that its author is not a movement-disorders neurologist, while displaying wide theoretical awareness of phenotypes, genetics and long-term strategy. [DOC]

And there sits the asymmetry.

Theoretical altitude rises.

The body becomes increasingly well classified.

But the relationship between the dystonic-looking lower limb, the painful abnormal feet, years of load-bearing and structural pathology remains curiously low-resolution.

The DisSynth will eventually offer possibilities: perhaps the leg symptoms, corns, calluses and burning were completely separate; perhaps peripheral neuropathy played a role; “Not clear.” [DOC]

That phrase can mean two very different things.

It can mean: we investigated the relationship properly and uncertainty remained.

Or it can mean: the relationship remained unclear because the investigation never fully formed around it.

Part I should end before deciding which.

End instead on the visual contradiction:

The hospital has searched the brain, the spine, the spleen, the genome and the future.

The patient is still standing there on the same two feet.

And the most basic question about how the abnormal neurological limb meets the ground remains unresolved.

Galway knew an extraordinary amount about what the patient might theoretically be. The unanswered question was whether it had looked closely enough at what the patient actually was.

INSTALLMENT II THE LAST OPEN DOOR The Protest and the choice to leave it at that +

The Protest and the choice to leave it at that

Part II should feel like time slowing down.

The first instalment establishes abundance: abundant history, abundant phenotype, abundant institutional capability, abundant curiosity directed toward Parkinsonism. The second asks what happened at the moment the remaining absence was identified by the person whose body carried it.

The key event is not a later complaint.

It is not retrospective criticism.

It is the patient, still an inpatient, saying in substance:

You have not dealt with my feet. [PATIENT]

Use the NCT / bald-tyres analogy in the patient's own remembered form once the exact wording is settled. [PATIENT — insert fullest account]

The point is not theatricality. The analogy is clinically intelligent. A car may have an engine diagnosis and still fail because the tyres are bald. A body may have Parkinsonism and still require somebody to explain why the part meeting the ground is painful, deformed and functionally decisive.

The Protest is the hinge because it turns omission into notice.

Before the Protest, the feet might have been deprioritised amid a dramatic young-onset Parkinsonian presentation.

After the Protest, the department cannot be described as simply unaware that the patient regarded the omission as important.

The patient has returned the missing thing to the centre.

Eight days of intellectual momentum

The timing matters.

By then, the case has momentum.

The neurological syndrome has been recognised. The department has discussed it. Imaging has been pursued. The possibility of atypical / recessive disease has been entertained. Senior treatment advice has entered the case. The long-term therapeutic future is being carefully narrated.

The admission is moving toward completion.

This is the moment at which an omission becomes most psychologically awkward.

Earlier in the week, reopening the feet would simply be another examination.

At the end, reopening the feet risks asking whether the thing now called a comprehensive admission is actually finished.

That is where commitment pressure belongs.

Do not call it pride.

Do not call it concealment.

Do not allege that anybody consciously thought, this will be embarrassing.

Write instead about structure.

The more intellectual work already invested in a formulation, the more disruptive a late contradiction can become. The greater the sophistication of the emerging synthesis, the more consequential it is to discover that the most disabling presenting problem has not been integrated.

The closer the admission came to completion, the more expensive curiosity became.

That is not a claim about character. It is a claim about how human institutions work.

The Tuesday decision and the narrowing of the experimental field

The chronology should establish, precisely, what happened at the Complex Case Meeting and what influence senior discussion had on therapy. [DEPT — verify date, attendees and source wording]

The record clearly shows that “the greatest of care” was advised with a senior consultant and neurological colleagues regarding future levodopa / dopamine-agonist treatment, with a strategy of avoiding those agents for as long as possible because of the patient's very young age and concerns about dyskinesia and impulse-control complications. [DOC]

This section must not mutate into an allegation that levodopa was mandatory in July 2017.

There were legitimate strategic reasons for caution in a very young patient.

The significance is different.

Once an obvious dopaminergic challenge is deferred, observation becomes more important. The service is electing to preserve a relatively untreated motor state while trying other symptomatic approaches. That makes the existing phenotype unusually valuable as a baseline.

The treatment decision narrows one experimental route while increasing the value of another: watch closely.

Watch the gait.

Watch the posture.

Watch the feet.

Watch what the alternative therapy changes.

Preserve what can still be seen before medication transforms it.

What the record saw

The record does see.

This distinction is essential.

The gait is videoed.

The pseudo-footdrop is described.

The possibility of dystonic posture is expressly raised.

Pes planus and hammertoes are noted.

The patient supplies the history of corns, calluses and foot treatment.

The DAT request itself says the walk is almost dystonically unusual. [DOC]

So this is not a story about a sign being completely invisible.

It is a story about a sign being seen, named, used diagnostically — and then not carried through into the synthesis of the painful foot problem.

That is much stronger.

The sign was not missed. It was seen. It was neurologically characterised. It was useful enough to appear in the diagnostic imagination. Then, when the feet themselves required synthesis, the line went dead.

Expertise enters the document

Now examine the DisSynth as an object.

It knows a lot.

It knows the thirteen-year possibility.

It knows the unusual phenotype.

It knows autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism.

It knows PRKN/PARK2.

It knows GBA / Gaucher possibilities.

It knows long-term treatment hazards in very young patients.

It knows enough to discuss future whole-genome or whole-exome analysis.

It knows enough to say prognosis may depend on genetic form.

It knows enough to describe the patient as unusually intelligent, cognitively excellent and capable of sophisticated engagement.

It knows enough to contemplate clinical trials and referral to colleagues in Dublin or the UK. [DOC]

And it tells us explicitly that the case has passed through more minds than one. It was discussed at the Neurology Complex Case Meeting. Senior consultant and colleague advice informed the treatment strategy. [DEPT]

Therefore, the serious hypothesis is not that one neurologist somehow invented all this sophistication alone.

The serious hypothesis is that departmental expertise uplifted the case theoretically.

That is ordinary medicine. Colleagues talk. Seniors advise. Cases are presented. A treating doctor learns from the room.

The problem appears one step later.

Where is the return loop?

The ideal process is:

observe → formulate → consult expertise → return to patient → test improved formulation → revise

The process suggested by the record may instead have been:

observe → formulate → consult expertise → improve theoretical synthesis → discharge

That is the missing bridge.

The expertise may have entered the document without entering the room.

More precisely: relevant expertise may have entered the interpretation of the case without being brought back to the unresolved foot–gait–pain relationship at the bedside.

This is the hypothesis to test, not a fact to assume.

Who contributed to the final synthesis?

Who supplied the PRKN reasoning?

Who shaped the long-term treatment caution?

Who attended the Complex Case Meeting?

Who actually re-examined the patient afterward?

Did anybody with movement-disorders expertise look at the feet dynamically?

Did anybody examine the soles?

Did anybody ask whether the callus pattern and painful sites corresponded to the abnormal gait?

Did anyone distinguish fixed deformity from dystonic posture?

Did the theoretical sophistication ever return to the body that generated it?

Two patients in one document

The DisSynth begins to look as though it contains two versions of the same man.

There is the observed patient.

He is grossly rigid. Bradykinetic. His walk is bizarre. One leg kicks out with a pseudo-footdrop appearance. His feet are structurally abnormal. He has a long bodily history.

Then there is the theoretical patient.

He may have young-onset idiopathic Parkinson's disease. Or a recessive form. PRKN is considered. GBA is considered. Atypical syndromes are considered. Therapeutic sensitivity is considered. Future genomics is considered.

The theoretical patient becomes increasingly sophisticated.

Then the document reaches the feet.

Perhaps separate.

Perhaps peripheral neuropathy.

Not clear.

Podiatry.

The contrast is startling.

The document knew the patient theoretically better than the documented investigation had established him physically.

This is what it means to say the DisSynth “betrays” its own process.

Not because the sophisticated material is false.

Because its sophistication creates an expectation of equivalent depth in the unresolved domains.

The more comprehensive the synthesis appears, the more important it becomes to ask what work earned each conclusion.

“Synthetic completeness” is not the same as “evidential completeness.”

A long letter can make uncertainty look investigated merely by placing it inside an impressive field of investigated things.

The patient detects the seam

Then comes the Protest.

The patient may not possess the vocabulary of phenotypic integration, dystonic biomechanics, information states or clinical epistemology.

He does not need it.

He knows the seam because he lives inside it.

The hospital has explained the engine.

The tyres are still bald.

The Protest is therefore not an awkward consumer complaint about service satisfaction.

It is diagnostic feedback.

The patient is telling the system that its model has failed a reality check.

This is especially striking because the DisSynth itself describes him as highly intelligent and “prescient,” able to say when information is overwhelming him. [DOC]

The same patient whose cognitive sophistication is celebrated in the document uses that sophistication to identify a hole in the admission.

And that hole is not carried into the finished account.

The two futures

At the Protest, two futures still exist.

Future A: Reopen

The response could have been:

You're right. We have diagnosed the Parkinsonian syndrome, but we have not properly answered the relationship between your neurological gait and the painful abnormal feet. Let's look again.

Then:

  • repeat examination;
  • shoes and socks off;
  • plantar surface documented;
  • dynamic gait re-examined with the painful area in mind;
  • movement-disorders expertise brought to bedside;
  • Podiatry asked a defined biomechanical / neurological question;
  • baseline images or video preserved;
  • uncertainty explicitly carried as an active problem;
  • planned review of foot state after procyclidine;
  • later dechallenge treated as information;
  • longitudinal follow-up integrated.

This path guarantees no answer.

That is important.

The defence against the manuscript cannot be: perhaps they still would not have known.

Of course perhaps they would not have known.

Science is allowed to remain uncertain.

The missed opportunity is not certainty.

It is structured curiosity.

Future B: Leave it at that

The admission remains conceptually complete.

Parkinsonism is the centre.

The feet may be separate.

Neuropathy is possible.

Not clear.

Podiatry.

Discharge.

The unresolved relationship is no longer a question the neurological service owns.

That is the fork.

The omitted Protest

The distinction from “omitted process” must be explicit.

The omission is the Protest itself.

The finished synthesis records an extraordinary amount about the patient's opinions, uncertainties, fears, intelligence, treatment preferences and understanding.

It records the feet.

It records Podiatry.

It records uncertainty.

But on the current record, it does not preserve the patient's end-of-admission challenge that the feet had not actually been dealt with. [PATIENT + OPEN — verify every discharge / clinic-letter version]

That omission changes the information state exported to everyone else.

There is an enormous difference between:

Feet remain unclear; Podiatry referral arranged.

and:

Feet remain unclear; patient has specifically objected before discharge that this principal presenting problem has not been dealt with.

The first sounds like managed uncertainty.

The second sounds like contested closure.

The first invites continuation.

The second should invite reconsideration.

The first separation was clinical: the feet from the phenotype. The second was informational: later clinicians from the fact that the patient had challenged that separation before it hardened.

The professional discomfort of reopening

This is where embarrassment can be discussed without alleging motive.

Suppose the Protest had produced a proper movement-disorders re-examination and that examination had found an obvious dynamic relationship between dystonia, loading and painful plantar pathology.

What would that mean?

It would not merely add a new fact.

It could expose that the institution had spent days performing sophisticated imaging, genetics and long-term treatment reasoning while under-investigating the patient's most disabling presenting complaint.

That is professionally uncomfortable.

The discomfort becomes greater in proportion to the expertise of the environment.

A university hospital with relevant dystonia, gait and young-onset Parkinsonism expertise has more intellectual capital at stake than a service with no such specialist tradition.

Again: no motive need be alleged.

The point is simply that, by the end of an admission, reopening can carry a cost that was not present at the beginning.

Embarrassment becomes structurally possible when the missing observation threatens the finishedness of an impressive explanation.

The door closes

Part II should end quietly.

Do not end with accusation.

End with loss.

The patient is discharged.

The raw phenomenon remains in his body.

The Protest does not travel with the formal account.

The uncertainty does travel — but as “Not clear,” not as a disputed and still-open failure of integration.

The institution has not solved the feet.

It has done something more consequential.

It has decided how future clinicians will first encounter them.

The door was still open. The patient pointed to it. Then it closed.

INSTALLMENT III THE PATIENT LEAVES; THE SYNTHESIS STAYS How one unresolved choice becomes an information architecture +

How one unresolved choice becomes an information architecture

A human being can leave a ward in a taxi.

A synthesis can keep moving for years.

That is the opening image of Part III.

The patient goes home with the same feet, the same soles, the same long natural history and a body now entering treatment. The hospital keeps the document. The GP receives the document. Nurses, podiatrists, later neurologists and anyone reviewing the historical case are more likely to meet the synthesised patient before they meet the raw July 2017 problem.

This is where “leave it at that” becomes larger than the discharge conversation.

It becomes infrastructure.

The portable patient

The July synthesis is unusually comprehensive. It is designed to consolidate discussion, explain the diagnosis and plan, and carry information outward. That is normally a virtue.

But the more authoritative a summary becomes, the more consequential its omissions and partitions become.

A later clinician does not usually begin by reconstructing the GP referral from scratch, re-reading every inpatient observation and interviewing the patient as if nobody has ever met him.

They begin with the inherited account.

Young-onset Parkinson's disease.

Atypical features.

Possible recessive genetics.

Long-term treatment strategy.

Feet: possibly separate / possible neuropathic element / Podiatry / not clear.

The document therefore becomes a kind of portable information state.

It does not merely report the patient.

It pre-classifies him.

The raw phenotype cannot travel easily between clinicians. The synthesis can.

That distinction will matter for every later observation.

The expertise problem changes shape

Patrick Browne belongs here, but with evidential discipline.

The working account is that Browne, whose professional role and publications place him squarely in the relevant movement-disorders environment, was away during the inpatient admission. [PATIENT / OPEN — documentary confirmation required]

The further working belief is that when he later encountered the case, he did so through essentially the same information state available to the GP: the completed synthesis rather than the unresolved raw admission. [PATIENT / OPEN — establish from correspondence and records]

If that is substantiated, the missed opportunity becomes almost perfectly clear.

The question is no longer:

Why did Patrick Browne fail to recognise the phenomenon?

It is:

Why did the patient never reach the relevant expertise in the information state in which that expertise could have mattered most?

Those are entirely different propositions.

An expert asked to see:

39-year-old, thirteen-year evolution, dystonic-looking gait, painful abnormal feet, unclear relationship — please examine before we close this

is being invited to discover.

An expert later handed:

young-onset Parkinson's; feet separately referred; relationship not clear

is being invited to manage.

The first question opens the case.

The second begins inside the answer.

By the time some expertise becomes available, the dinosaur may already have been converted into a discharge summary.

Procyclidine: the body begins answering

Now treatment enters.

This is where the manuscript should become almost cinematic because the core idea is so simple.

The hospital did not design a prospective foot experiment.

The body did.

Procyclidine is introduced.

The patient's movement improves. His lived experience changes. The feet change with it. [RX — insert precise 2017 patient reports]

The patient has every reason to feel reassured.

He has been told he has Parkinson's disease. He has been given neurological medication. He feels better. The new framework appears to work.

That is the human asymmetry at the centre of the story:

The treatment response reassures the patient at exactly the same time that it should increase professional curiosity about the disputed boundary.

The patient does not need to infer mechanism. He has specialists for that.

He reports what happens.

They are the people responsible for deciding what it means.

The dechallenge

Then comes one of the most important later observations.

In October 2017, during a planned break from procyclidine, the patient's own email reports that the walk begins to fall apart again and the feet become restless as tension builds. [RX — insert exact email]

By April 2018, the treating neurologist records that when procyclidine had been stopped for two weeks, the first thing noticed changing rapidly was the return of the outside-left-foot corn phenomenon. [RX — insert exact contemporaneous wording]

Handle this scientifically.

Do not write that an established structural corn literally vanished and reappeared pharmacologically. Hyperkeratotic lesions do not dematerialise on command.

The observation requires interpretation.

What exactly returned rapidly?

Pain?

Pressure?

Awareness of the lesion?

A loading pattern?

Dystonic posture changing the local mechanical stress?

The point is not that 2018 proves one mechanism.

The point is that a neurological medication withdrawal produced a rapid, focal event at the very foot territory that had been left “Not clear.”

That should have been scientifically delicious.

The body had crossed the conceptual border the document had drawn.

The body reopened the 2017 question pharmacologically. The formulation did not reopen with it.

This is the moment to explain why a patient can live through such evidence without recognising its full significance.

He experiences the phenomenon directly.
