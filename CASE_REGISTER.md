# Case Register

**Status:** CONTROLLING PRESENTATION / ANALYTICAL REGISTER

**Purpose:** Keep the case alive in ordinary language while separating what the record says from what we infer, argue, or ask an expert to decide.

**Canonical source:** `SRC_COMPLETE_RECORD_FORENSIC.md` controls every factual dispute.

---

# 1. The rule

Use this order:

> **Quote first.  
> Fact second.  
> Proposition third.  
> Argument fourth.  
> Expert opinion fifth.  
> Law last.**

Do not turn one layer into another.

A quote is not automatically a fact about mechanism.

A fact is not automatically negligence.

A proposition is not automatically an expert conclusion.

An argument is not evidence.

Later evidence must not be pushed backwards into an earlier date.

---

# 2. The centre of the case

> **Unresolved source of disabling pain**  
> **becomes**  
> **specialist uncertainty about a possibly separate foot problem.**

Then something new happens:

> **Neurological tablets change what happens in the feet.**

The simple question becomes:

> **If the tablets given for the neurological condition could change the foot problem, why did that not make Neurology ask the original question again?**

# 4. Facts — what the record directly supports

These are documentary facts, not negligence findings.

1. The patient had longstanding foot pain before neurological treatment.
2. Primary care documented real structural foot disease before the Neurology admission.
3. Primary care also documented abnormal gait, increased tone, movement difficulty and functional decline.
4. The urgent referral placed abnormal feet and abnormal movement in the same clinical presentation.
5. Neurology identified severe young-onset Parkinsonism.
6. Neurology described the gait as strikingly abnormal.
7. Neurology described a foot-drop-like appearance as suggestive of dystonic posture and expressly not true foot drop.
8. Neurology documented structural foot findings including pes planus and hammertoes.
9. The neurological examination was videoed.
10. Physiotherapy and Occupational Therapy were listed as involved.
11. Podiatry was already a live care pathway and was appropriate for genuine structural foot problems.
12. The GP told Podiatry the case would be prioritised “as Parkinson's.”
13. The final specialist account said the foot / leg symptoms may have been completely separate, possibly neuropathic, and “Not clear.”
14. The complete record does not preserve a clear written account showing how the abnormal neurological lower limb was tested against the painful structural feet before that separation language was used.
15. The record does not contain an adequate documented formal foot sensory / reflex set sufficient on its face to explain the neuropathy branch; absence of documentation does not prove the tests were not performed.
16. The project treats the pre-discharge Protest — **“You have not dealt with my feet”** — as fact. That exact phrase is not located verbatim in the canonical source; later patient-authored material records that he raised disabling bilateral foot pain as unaddressed and asked for the supervising neurologist.
17. In October 2017, during a planned procyclidine withdrawal, the patient contemporaneously reported worsening tension, restless feet and deteriorating walking.
18. In April 2018, the treating neurologist recorded that a focal outside-left-foot phenomenon had changed / returned rapidly during that earlier withdrawal.
19. The April 2018 note records the earlier event; it is not a second withdrawal experiment.
20. In March 2019, primary care expressly linked foot problems to increased tone, abnormal gait and Parkinson's disease.
21. The available Podiatry record shows 25 March 2019 as the last recorded attendance; it does not prove that every later Podiatry allocation ceased to exist.
22. Later Neurology itself repeatedly recorded foot movement, foot sensitivity, morning foot problems and severe pain.
23. In 2023, a Neurology review raised Parkinsonian dystonia, more likely OFF dystonia.
24. A later Movement Disorders contact said the patient appeared undertreated and that leg pain and shuffling were OFF-related.
25. The 2023 record also contains contrary patient-reported evidence against a simple Parkinson-related explanation.
26. By 2025 the treating record expressly accepted that the feet felt better from a pain perspective when Sinemet reached the system.
27. The same 2025 record also maintained that the described pain was not direct Parkinson's phenomenology and raised the trade-off between further pain relief and worse dyskinesia.
28. The formal complaint entered GUH governance in 2025 and eventually produced a SIMT conclusion of “no deficit in the care provided.”
29. The Podiatry service was part of Community Healthcare West rather than a GUH hospital service.
30. The February 2026 St Vincent's letter directly states that Parkin mutations account for the patient's Parkinsonism and dystonia.

# 5. Propositions — reasoned statements drawn from the facts

These are not direct quotations and are not yet expert conclusions.

1. **The source of the disabling foot pain remained unresolved in 2017.**
2. **The specialist record nevertheless made it possible for the foot problem to proceed as something separate from the main neurological explanation.**
3. **Seeing a dystonic-looking lower limb is not the same as answering whether that abnormal movement was affecting the painful feet.**
4. **Real structural foot disease does not by itself show that the neurological disorder played no part in posture, loading or pain.**
5. **Different behaviour of two symptoms does not by itself prove different causes.**
6. **The October 2017 / April 2018 medication evidence made the feet and Neurology harder to treat as unrelated in practice, even though it did not prove one mechanism.**
7. **By 2019 primary care was again treating feet, tone and gait as connected enough to mention together.**
8. **After the last recorded Podiatry attendance, Neurology increasingly had the foot problem directly in front of it again.**
9. **The later record contains genuine reassessment and should not be described as eight years of total inaction.**
10. **The later record nevertheless repeatedly raises the question of whether the original source-of-pain problem was ever rebuilt from the beginning.**
11. **The system accumulated more information over time, but accumulation of information is not necessarily the same as putting the information together.**
12. **The neurological medication did not create the original pain, but the record supports asking whether treatment state affected how severe or disabling the pain became.**

# 6. Arguments — claimant-side positions to be tested

These are arguments. They must never be presented as source facts.

## Argument 1 — the 2017 assessment did not earn the separation

> **They saw the abnormal walk. They knew the feet hurt. The record does not show enough work explaining why the two could safely be treated as separate.**

## Argument 2 — neuropathy was allowed to do too much explanatory work

> **If peripheral neuropathy was going to explain the burning feet, the record should show the clinical evidence that supported it.**

## Argument 3 — the Protest should have changed what happened next

> **Once the patient said the feet had not been dealt with, the unfinished question should have been answered, explained or clearly handed over before closure.**

## Argument 4 — Podiatry did not answer the neurological question

> **Podiatry could properly treat corns, calluses and loading problems. Someone still had to ask what the abnormal neurological gait was doing to the feet.**

## Argument 5 — the tablets should have forced a fresh look

> **The tablets prescribed for the neurological condition changed what happened in the feet. When they were stopped, the foot problem changed quickly. That should have made Neurology ask again whether the foot problem was really separate.**

## Argument 6 — later evidence repeatedly reopened the same simple question

> **As foot movement, morning difficulty, OFF-related pain, toe curling and Sinemet-related pain relief accumulated, the old separation became harder to leave untested.**

## Argument 7 — the harm case is about suffering, not wording

> **If earlier understanding or better treatment would probably have reduced pain, abnormal loading, loss of function or psychological distress, that is the injury. The injury is not the wording of the record itself.**

## Argument 8 — the institutional problem is one of ownership

> **Different services held different pieces. The question is who was responsible, at each date, for putting enough of those pieces together to act.**

## Argument 9 — governance has to be tested against what it actually reviewed

> **A later conclusion of “no deficit” is only as strong as the material and questions the review actually considered. The complete PAR evidence pack is not presently in the reviewed corpus.**

# 7. Expert information — what a clinical expert must decide

The project should not answer these questions for the expert.

## 2017 duty and examination

1. What examination should reasonable Neurology have performed to work out whether the neurological disorder was contributing to the painful feet?
2. Was the documented examination enough to support the statement that the foot problem might be completely separate?
3. What positive clinical findings, if any, supported peripheral neuropathy?
4. Which of the specifically undocumented examination domains would reasonable practice actually have required, or what equivalent work would have been sufficient?
5. If the relationship could not be answered in July 2017, should the question have remained actively open?

## Protest and handoff

6. Assuming the Protest occurred as the project records it, what should reasonable Neurology have done next?
7. What information should have gone to Podiatry beyond the Parkinson's label?
8. Was a defined feedback / return plan needed between Neurology, primary care and Community Healthcare West Podiatry?

## Medication evidence

9. What clinical significance should have been attached to the October 2017 deterioration when procyclidine was stopped?
10. What significance should have been attached, by April 2018, to the treating neurologist's own record of a rapidly returning focal foot phenomenon during that withdrawal?
11. Did those facts require a fresh examination or change in the working explanation even if the original July assessment had been reasonable?

## Later years

12. What is the significance of the March 2019 referral linking feet, tone and abnormal gait?
13. At what later point, if any, did the accumulation of foot, gait and medication evidence require a fresh review of the feet and neurological disorder together?
14. How should the expert treat the contrary 2023 evidence, including the patient's own belief that the pain was not Parkinson-related and his report of no perceived difference after stopping medication?
15. What is the significance of the later OFF-dystonia / undertreatment observations?
16. What is the significance of the 2025 acknowledgement that Sinemet made the feet feel better from a pain perspective while the clinician still considered the pain not directly Parkinsonian?

## Harm and causation

17. Did delayed understanding or treatment probably cause extra pain, loading injury, loss of mobility or loss of function?
18. Did prolonged severe pain probably contribute to later psychological distress?
19. What part of the later suffering would probably have happened anyway because of the underlying neurological disease, genuine structural foot disease or treatment side effects?
20. For every breach found, what would better care probably have changed, and from what date?

# 10. The legal structure underneath

## Duty

> **What should reasonable care have done with the information available at that date?**

## Breach

> **What should have been done but was not?**

## Harm

> **What extra suffering or injury followed?**

## Causation

> **If the right thing had been done at the right time, what would probably have changed?**

That legal structure controls the analysis. It should not control the voice.
