# Factual Spine

**Status:** CORE FACTUAL FOUNDATION — STRONGER FORMULATION

**Function:** This is the compact factual document supporting the organising architecture. It is not a pleading, expert report, negligence finding or causation opinion.

The governing factual question is:

> **What happened to the integrated feet–gait–tone–movement problem after it entered Neurology, what work is actually demonstrated on the relationship among those components, how did “may have been completely separate” and “markedly divorced” enter the final specialist account, what happened when the Podiatry allocation later ended, and what later facts were capable of testing that founding allocation across the hospital?**

Later evidence must not be used to rewrite earlier knowledge.

---

# 1. The integrated problem before Neurology

## 10 April 2017

Primary care documented abnormal gait, bilateral foot pain, marked mallet toes, onychogryphosis, callus / pressure areas and difficulty working on the feet. Podiatry was planned.

This establishes genuine structural foot pathology before neurological diagnosis.

## 8 June 2017

The GP considered Neurology specifically for:

> **gait, feet and “muscle tension” issues.**

Difficulty initiating movement was already present.

## 23 June 2017

The GP documented marked increased tone in both legs, brisk upper-limb reflexes, gait-related work difficulty and alcohol being used to help “relax muscles.”

## 29 June 2017 urgent referral

The urgent referral combined bilateral foot problems, an unusual festinant / stiff gait, difficulty initiating movement, marked stiffness and increased tone in upper and lower limbs, brisk reflexes, possible cogwheeling, toe clawing, inability to relax the muscles in bed, worsening dexterity and deteriorating work function.

The feet did not arrive as a problem already proved independent of the neurological presentation.

> **painful abnormal feet + abnormal gait + pathological tone / movement disorder.**

---

# 2. What Neurology actually detected

The July neurological assessment identified a substantial Parkinsonian syndrome.

Documented findings included markedly increased and relatively symmetrical tone, lead-pipe and axial rigidity, gross bradykinesia, absent arm swing, markedly reduced blink rate, strikingly abnormal gait, a left lower-limb / foot-drop-like appearance, posture suggestive of dystonia and expressly **“NOT FOOT DROP”**, pes planus and hammertoes.

The examination was video-recorded.

The differential included very-young-onset Parkinsonism and possible autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism.

> **The lower-limb sign was seen and neurologically characterised.**

The later issue is what work is demonstrated connecting that sign to the painful abnormal feet.

---

# 3. What the July record demonstrates about the feet

The record contains a history of longstanding corns and calluses, fallen arches, treatment since approximately 2009–2010, foot pain affected by occupational standing exposure, persistent corns / calluses even when foot pain became less prominent, and a historical subjective distinction between foot pain and bodily / leg tension.

The neurological examination records pes planus and hammertoes.

The short discharge material records:

> **Podiatry referral has been sent to review issues with feet reported by the patient.**

What the presently identified July neurological material does **not clearly demonstrate** is directed mapping of painful foot sites against abnormal gait, a documented plantar / load-bearing examination directed at the painful lesions, fixed-versus-dynamic foot / toe analysis, a documented examination of whether dystonic posture or pathological tone altered loading, a defined neurological biomechanical formulation, a clearly documented movement-disorders examination of the feet, or a distinct Neurology-to-Podiatry question about dystonia, tone, gait, posture, loading or the structural–dynamic relationship.

This is a documentary finding only.

> **Do not convert absence of identified documentation into proof that no examination occurred.**

---

# 4. The patient’s historical distinction: distinguishable does not equal independent

The patient had noticed that foot pain and bodily / leg tension did not behave identically. After leaving bar work and spending less time on his feet, foot pain reduced while bodily tension persisted.

What that directly establishes is:

> **the patient experienced the foot pain and bodily tension as distinguishable phenomena.**

It does not by itself establish separate causation, absence of neurological influence on loading, absence of a mixed mechanism or neurological independence of the structural foot pathology.

---

# 5. “May have been completely separate”

The final specialist synthesis states that the leg / foot symptoms including corns, callouses and burning:

> **“may have been completely separate”**

or perhaps represented a secondary peripheral neuropathy, possibly related in some respect to prior alcohol intake, and concludes:

> **“Not clear.”**

This is the first explicit specialist wording in the final synthesis giving the unresolved relationship a possible state of **complete separation**.

- **“Not clear”** records uncertainty;
- **“may have been completely separate”** identifies separation as an explanatory possibility;
- Podiatry supplies the practical destination.

The source record does not itself show that “complete separation” had been established by a directed relationship-specific examination.

---

# 6. “Markedly divorced”

Immediately after the “completely separate / peripheral neuropathy / Not clear” passage, the final synthesis states that the patient had:

> **“in his own mind - markedly divorced”**

his feelings of bodily / leg tension from the corns, callouses and pains in the feet.

The underlying history was a difference in symptom behaviour over time and with standing exposure.

The documentary distinction is:

> **patient describes non-identical experiences**

versus

> **specialist text records the patient as having “markedly divorced” the two problem domains.**

Whether that was a sound clinical basis for mechanistic separation is an expert question.

---

# 7. The Protest

The project treats the pre-discharge Protest as fact.

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

It occurred while the patient was still available for examination and before he had seen the later completed specialist synthesis.

The Protest does not create the earlier documentary question. It makes the alleged incompleteness explicit before closure.

---

# 8. The discharge lifecycle and founding hospital state

Physical discharge occurred on **11 July 2017**. The DaT scan was performed on **13 July 2017** and was abnormal. Formal summary confirmation occurred on **17 July 2017 at 19:30**. On 18 July the GP recorded receipt of extensive correspondence and coded Parkinson’s disease in primary care.

The final document contained high-resolution neurological / neurogenetic reasoning alongside:

> **“may have been completely separate”**

> **“Not clear”**

> **“markedly divorced”**

and Podiatry as the foot destination.

The final account does not preserve the Protest.

The founding hospital state is therefore factually describable as:

> **neurological syndrome strongly formulated + feet separately directed + relationship left uncertain.**

---

# 9. Brennan’s downstream information state

Brennan knew the integrated patient before admission but did not witness the neurological bedside work.

The specialist account did not tell him whether the relationship had been adequately investigated and remained uncertain, or whether it remained uncertain because the necessary relationship-directed work had not been completed before separation.

Nor did it preserve the Protest.

This is the factual foundation for the later expert question about what a reasonable GP would understand from the specialist communication.

---

# 10. Treatment restraint and baseline value

The July plan deliberately deferred conventional dopaminergic treatment because of the patient’s young age and long-term treatment risks. Rasagiline and procyclidine were used as early symptomatic treatments. Later correspondence records:

> **“Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa…”**

Prof Daniel Healy was asked for advice.

The factual significance is that treatment restraint was an active strategy and that the relatively untreated / minimally treated phenotype had observational value before later treatment altered it.

---

# 11. September–October 2017 — first treatment-state crossing

Prof Daniel Healy recorded probable **PARK II** and substantial improvement on procyclidine / rasagiline. His opinion belongs to September and must not be projected backwards into July.

During procyclidine withdrawal the patient contemporaneously reported:

> **“tension building up, my feet are restless and my walk is falling apart...”**

This established that neurological treatment state altered phenomena involving bodily tension, the feet and walking. It did not establish one mechanism.

---

# 12. September 2017–March 2019 — Podiatry creates the mechanical archive

Podiatry documented real focal mechanical / structural pathology, especially at the **left fifth MTPJ**. The record includes recurrent callus / corn / HD, clawed toes, tight gastrocnemius / soleus, pronating foot type, debridement / enucleation, toe props, direct offloading and insoles.

The pathology was real.

> **The Podiatry record establishes where mechanical loading produced morbidity.**

It does not by itself establish why the loading pattern existed or whether neurological posture / gait materially influenced it.

Neurology and Podiatry therefore accumulated different information about the same lower-limb system.

---

# 13. April 2018 — independent reopening evidence

In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded that during the earlier withdrawal the first thing changing rapidly was the return on the foot of the **outside-left-foot / corn phenomenon**. The same note recorded broader worsening including greater shiftiness / lack of relaxation off procyclidine.

Do not infer that a structural corn literally disappeared and reappeared pharmacologically.

> **Neurology documented a rapid focal foot-state change during neurological medication withdrawal in the same broad lateral-foot territory that Podiatry was mechanically treating.**

That observation existed independently of the July Protest and independently of whether July uncertainty had initially been reasonable.

---

# 14. March 2019 — critical institutional transition

A March 2019 GP referral again linked:

- feet;
- increased tone;
- abnormal gait;
- Parkinson’s disease;
- clawing;
- pain.

The Podiatry record identifies:

> **Patient Last Seen: 25 Mar 2019.**

This is a critical transition in the hospital-wide history.

Before that date there is an active operational split: Neurology carries the movement disorder while Podiatry actively carries the structural feet.

After that date, the available Podiatry record no longer shows an active Podiatry treatment stream.

Therefore the later history should not be described as though the feet remained continuously allocated to Podiatry.

> **After March 2019, the operational allocation disappears but the conceptual partition survives.**

This is especially important because primary care itself had just re-integrated feet + tone + abnormal gait + Parkinson’s disease.

---

# 15. 2020–2022 — Neurology directly reacquires the feet

## 2020

Neurology recorded a lower-limb **“foot throw”** resembling foot drop but classified it as **Parkinsonian** because dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion power were normal.

The same note recorded excessive foot movement and exquisite foot sensitivity.

There was no longer an active Podiatry stream in the available record. Neurology itself now directly possessed neurological foot findings.

## 2021

Sinemet was introduced. Dyskinesia / chorea risk became clinically relevant.

## 2022

The patient reported that the feet took approximately **20–25 minutes to “warm up”** in the morning and remained sore around corns / calluses.

This was a reproducible state / time-linked observation.

These later facts do not prove mechanism. They establish repeated post-Podiatry opportunities for Neurology to consider the original relationship directly.

---

# 16. 2023 — the integrated problem returns unmistakably to Neurology

The GP urgently referred severe bilateral burning foot / lower-leg pain and noted that a previous severe pain episode had suddenly settled with better Parkinson’s control.

Neurology’s 2023 record includes severe foot pain, mobility restriction, possible OFF dystonia, gait deterioration, neuropathic possibilities, Fabry overlap investigation and treatment-state analysis.

A Movement Disorders nurse assessed leg pain and shuffling as **OFF-related** and considered the patient undertreated.

The consultant described a **“misalignment”** between his own interpretation and the patient’s belief that a major separate foot problem existed, while recording that the patient’s concerns were reasonable / justifiable.

A contrary fact remains important: the patient at one point reported stopping Parkinson medication for ten days and feeling no Parkinson difference, while believing the debilitating pain was not Parkinson-related.

The later record is therefore mixed, not one-directional.

The factual institutional point is narrower:

> **By 2023 the feet, dystonia / OFF phenomena, gait and treatment state were all directly present inside Neurology again, without an active Podiatry owner in the available record.**

---

# 17. 2024–2025 — increasingly detailed mixed lower-limb evidence

## 2024

Pregabalin reduced pain while activity-dependent toe curling persisted.

This separated at least some pain experience from the motor toe-curling phenomenon.

## Early 2025

Neurology recorded that foot pain was the dominant concern; the first footsteps were extremely painful; once Parkinson’s medication reached the system the feet felt better from a pain perspective; and additional Sinemet would probably provide additional pain relief, limited by dyskinesia risk.

The clinician continued to distinguish the underlying pain from direct Parkinsonian phenomenology and acknowledged that dystonia can be painful.

The later record could therefore simultaneously contain dopaminergic modulation of foot pain, dystonic / motor foot phenomena, structural / local pain and a separate-pain explanatory model.

---

# 18. March–September 2025 — the clinical issue becomes an institutional governance issue

In March 2025 the hospital was expressly notified of the historical allegation that the feet had not been properly integrated into the neurological case.

The subsequent governance history included QPS complaint handling and later Incident Management Framework / PAR / SIMT review.

The record later describes the SIMT outcome as:

> **“no deficit in care.”**

This creates a distinct institutional information state.

The factual significance is not that governance thereby independently proved every founding clinical proposition. It is that the historical care question was no longer merely inherited by individual clinicians; it was placed before hospital governance for review.

---

# 19. December 2025–January 2026 — governance is asked to reconsider and continues to rely on the completed state

The preserved corpus records that the patient reported a specialist diagnosis of **dystonia** on 19 December 2025. The primary December specialist letter has not been located in the reviewed corpus and remains an evidential gap.

The patient then re-engaged QPS in January 2026, expressly asking about present governance consequences rather than simply reopening the complaint.

On 26 January 2026 QPS stated that the complaint assessment and subsequent SIMT review had been undertaken using the information, clinical presentations and investigations available at the time; that the December 2025 dystonia diagnosis did not undermine the validity of those processes or outcomes; and that reopening governance reviews, altering prior determinations or retrospectively amending historical records was not considered necessary.

The factual institutional transition is:

> **clinical inheritance → express institutional review → continued governance reliance.**

This is different from passive downstream clinical inheritance.

# 20. February 2026 — genetic clarification

The St Vincent’s genetic-results letter states:

> **two pathogenic variants in the Parkin gene**

and concludes that the patient’s:

> **Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous mutations in the Parkin gene.**

This is later clarification only. It must not be used to rewrite what was knowable or required in July 2017.

Its longitudinal significance is that the neurological phenotype ultimately integrates both Parkinsonism and dystonia genetically.

That sharpens, but does not answer, the historical question concerning the painful feet.
