# Expert Brief — Duty, Breach, Harm and Causation

**Purpose:** Give the expert the factual sequence in ordinary language and ask the clinical questions needed for duty, breach, harm and causation.

**Important:** `SRC_COMPLETE_RECORD_FORENSIC.md` controls the facts. `CASE_REGISTER.md` separates quotations, facts, propositions, arguments and expert questions.

The expert is not asked to adopt the claimant's theory. The expert is asked to test it.

---

# 1. The case in plain language

> **The patient had disabling foot pain. Neurology found a very abnormal neurological gait. The source of the pain remained unresolved. The specialist record then allowed the feet to sit as a possibly separate problem. Later neurological medication changed what happened in the feet. The expert question is what reasonable Neurology should have done with those facts, and what difference better care would probably have made.**

The expert is **not** being asked to prove that every foot lesion was neurological or that all pain was dystonia.

---

# 2. What Neurology received in 2017

Primary care sent a patient with:

- painful abnormal feet;
- abnormal gait;
- increased tone / stiffness;
- difficulty starting movement;
- toe clawing;
- declining function.

Podiatry was already involved for real structural foot disease.

Neurology then found severe young-onset Parkinsonism and described the gait as strikingly abnormal, with a foot-drop-like appearance suggestive of dystonic posture but not true foot drop.

The simple clinical question was:

> **Was the neurological disorder contributing to what was happening in the feet?**

---

# 3. Duty — what should reasonable Neurology have done?

Please address:

1. What clinical problem did Neurology receive in 2017?
2. Did disabling foot pain plus abnormal gait, increased tone, toe clawing and the abnormal lower limb require Neurology to investigate whether the neurological disorder was contributing to the feet?
3. What examination would reasonable practice normally require before treating the feet as separate?
4. If the connection could not be answered, should the question have stayed open?
5. Even if Podiatry properly treated the structural lesions, did Neurology need to retain responsibility for the neurological part of the foot problem?
6. What should have happened when later neurological medication changed what happened in the feet?

---

# 4. Possible breach — founding assessment

> **Was enough done in July 2017 to work out whether the neurological disorder was contributing to the painful feet?**

If not, please identify what should have been done.

The issue is not whether Neurology saw the abnormal gait. It did.

The issue is:

> **What examination connected that abnormal gait to the painful feet, or showed that they were independent?**

---

# 5. Possible breach — treating the feet as separate

The final specialist account said the symptoms **“may have been completely separate”** and also said **“Not clear.”**

Please answer:

> **Was there enough clinical evidence to justify treating complete separation as a reasonable explanation?**

If yes, identify the examination or reasoning that supported it.

If no, explain what further work should have been done.

---

# 6. Possible breach — peripheral neuropathy

Peripheral neuropathy was raised as another explanation.

Please answer:

> **What positive clinical evidence supported neuropathy?**

Would reasonable practice have required formal sensory examination, reflex testing or other work before neuropathy could reasonably explain the burning foot symptoms?

The complete-record review identifies a number of examination items for which no adequate written counterpart has been found. Lack of documentation does not prove that the examinations were not performed.

The expert should identify which examinations, or equivalent work, were actually required.

---

# 7. The Protest

The project treats the pre-discharge Protest as fact:

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

The exact phrase is not located verbatim in the canonical record. Later patient-authored material records that he raised disabling bilateral foot pain as unaddressed and asked for the supervising neurologist.

Assuming the Protest occurred, please answer:

> **What should reasonable Neurology have done next?**

Would reasonable care have required further examination, explanation, senior review, a clear follow-up plan, or some other response?

---

# 8. Podiatry and the handoff

Podiatry was appropriate for genuine structural foot disease.

The GP also recorded that the Podiatry case would be prioritised **“as Parkinson's.”**

The question is not whether Podiatry should have been involved.

It is:

> **Who was responsible for making sure the neurological findings and the Podiatry findings were put back together?**

Please address whether reasonable care required a defined question, feedback route or return plan between Neurology, primary care and Podiatry.

The later record confirms that the relevant Podiatry service was part of Community Healthcare West rather than a GUH hospital department.

---

# 9. The medication evidence

The foot pain existed before neurological treatment. Neurology did not create the original pain.

During a planned procyclidine withdrawal in October 2017 the patient contemporaneously reported:

> **“I am feeling the tension building up, my feet are restless and my walk is falling apart...”**

A later April 2018 neurology note recorded that during that earlier withdrawal:

> **“the first thing he noticed changing and changing rapidly was the return on the foot, of the outside left corn.”**

Do not treat the April note as a second withdrawal experiment, and do not assume a structural corn literally disappeared and reappeared because of medication.

Please answer:

1. What clinical significance should have been attached to the October 2017 deterioration?
2. By April 2018, what significance should have been attached to the treating neurologist's own record of a rapid focal foot change during withdrawal?
3. Did these facts require the original foot–neurology question to be looked at again, even if July 2017 care had initially been reasonable?

---

# 10. Later years

Later Neurology itself recorded or received:

- Parkinsonian-looking foot movement;
- foot sensitivity and excess movement;
- morning foot problems;
- severe bilateral pain;
- possible OFF dystonia;
- a Movement Disorders view that leg pain and shuffling were OFF-related and the patient appeared undertreated;
- toe curling;
- pregabalin benefit;
- Sinemet benefit from a pain perspective.

The record also contains contrary evidence. At one stage the patient thought the pain was not Parkinson-related and reported no perceived Parkinson difference after stopping medication for ten days.

Please do not flatten this into a one-sided story.

The expert question is:

> **At what point, if any, did the later information require a fresh review of the feet and neurological disorder together?**

---

# 11. Do not import later diagnoses backwards

In 2017 the record used descriptions such as **“dystonic posture”** and **“dystonically unusual”** for the gait.

That is not the same as saying the foot pain was diagnosed as dystonia.

Later clinicians used dystonia more explicitly. In February 2026 a St Vincent's genetic letter stated that Parkin mutations accounted for the patient's Parkinsonism and dystonia.

Keep those later facts out of the 2017 standard-of-care question.

The 2017 question is simply:

> **What should reasonable Neurology have done with painful feet plus a very abnormal neurological lower limb?**

---

# 12. Harm — what injury may have followed?

The relevant harm is not wording on paper by itself.

Please consider whether any breach probably caused extra:

- foot pain;
- difficulty walking or standing;
- abnormal loading or recurrent structural foot injury;
- loss of mobility or function;
- loss of work or ordinary activity;
- psychological distress caused by prolonged severe pain.

Please separate any harm caused by breach from harm that would have happened anyway because of the underlying neurological disease, genuine structural foot disease or treatment side effects.

---

# 13. Psychological deterioration

By 2023 the record contains severe bilateral foot / lower-leg pain, an urgent GP concern that the pain was causing a death wish, and a later nursing record of debilitating pain and no quality of life.

The expert is not asked to say Neurology caused the original pain.

The narrower question is:

> **Did neurological treatment materially affect the severity of the pre-existing pain, and did any failure to understand or optimise that connection contribute to prolonged severe pain and later psychological distress?**

Please identify each causal step that can be supported and each step that cannot.

---

# 14. Causation — what difference would better care probably have made?

For each breach you find, please answer:

1. What should have been done instead?
2. What would it probably have found?
3. Would that probably have changed the explanation of the foot problem?
4. Would that probably have changed treatment or follow-up?
5. Would that probably have reduced pain, loading problems, loss of function or distress?
6. From what date?
7. How much of the later harm would probably have happened anyway?

Please label each causal conclusion as:

- probable;
- possible only;
- or indeterminate.

---

# 15. Primary care

The GP sent a combined feet / gait / tone / movement problem into Neurology and later received a specialist account in which the feet could be separate, neuropathic or “Not clear.”

Please consider:

> **Would a reasonable GP reading that account understand that the foot–neurology question had already been adequately investigated?**

If yes, and if it had not actually been adequately investigated, could the specialist account have delayed later reconsideration?

---

# 16. Institutional question

Different services held different pieces of the problem.

Do not assume that “the hospital” automatically knew everything every clinician knew.

Ask instead:

> **At each important date, who had enough information to ask the next obvious question, and what should reasonable care have required them to do?**

---

# 17. Required answer format

For every breach found, please state:

- date;
- information available then;
- what reasonable care required;
- what was not done;
- why that fell below standard;
- what should have happened instead;
- what would probably have changed;
- what harm would probably have been reduced;
- what harm would have happened anyway;
- confidence level.

---

# 18. Final instruction

> **Do not let technical language hide the factual sequence. The patient had disabling foot pain. Neurology found a very abnormal neurological gait. Neurological treatment later changed what happened in the feet. Decide what reasonable Neurology should have done with those facts and what difference better care would probably have made.**
