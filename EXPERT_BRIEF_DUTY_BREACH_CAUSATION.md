# Expert Brief — Duty, Breach, Harm and Causation

**Purpose:** Ask the expert the simplest questions the record raises, while keeping the legal structure exact.

The expert should not be asked to adopt the claimant's theory. The expert should be asked to test it.

---

# 1. The case in plain language

> **The patient had disabling foot pain. Neurology found a very abnormal neurological gait. The source of the pain remained unresolved. The specialist record then allowed the feet to sit as a possibly separate problem. Later neurological medication changed the foot problem. The question is what reasonable Neurology should have done with those facts, and what difference better care would probably have made.**

The expert is **not** being asked to prove that every foot lesion was neurological or that all pain was dystonia.

---

# 2. Duty — what should reasonable Neurology have done?

Please address:

1. **What clinical problem did Neurology receive in 2017?**
2. **Did the combination of disabling foot pain, abnormal gait, increased tone, toe clawing and a dystonic-looking lower limb require Neurology to ask whether the neurological disorder was contributing to the foot problem?**
3. **What examination would reasonable practice normally require before treating the feet as separate?**
4. **If the relationship could not be answered, should the question have remained open rather than being allowed to fall out of the neurological formulation?**
5. **Did Neurology need to retain responsibility for the relationship even if Podiatry treated the structural lesions?**
6. **What should have happened when later neurological treatment changed the foot problem?**

---

# 3. Breach — what may have gone wrong?

Please consider each possible breach separately.

## A. Founding assessment

> **Was enough done in July 2017 to work out whether the neurological disorder was contributing to the painful feet?**

If not, what was missing?

## B. Treating the feet as separate

> **Was there enough clinical evidence to justify saying the foot problem might be completely separate?**

What examination or reasoning supported that step?

## C. Peripheral neuropathy

> **What positive clinical evidence supported peripheral neuropathy as an explanation?**

Would reasonable practice normally have required formal sensory / reflex examination or other testing?

## D. The Protest

Assume the patient said in substance:

> **You have not dealt with my feet.**

What should reasonable Neurology have done next?

## E. Podiatry handoff

Podiatry was a proper service for real structural foot disease.

The question is:

> **Who was responsible for making sure the neurological and Podiatry findings were put back together?**

Was a defined return / feedback plan required?

## F. Medication response

The record later shows that neurological medication changed the patient's foot state and that stopping procyclidine was followed by rapid return of a focal foot problem.

> **Did that require the original question to be reopened?**

If not, why not?

## G. Later years

As Neurology later recorded foot movement, morning foot problems, severe pain, possible OFF dystonia, OFF-related pain / shuffling, toe curling and pain benefit from Sinemet:

> **At what point, if any, did reasonable care require the original relationship to be rebuilt from the beginning?**

---

# 4. The examination gap

A conservative review of the complete record finds no adequate documentary counterpart for a number of potentially relevant examinations, including formal foot sensation, vibration, joint position, Achilles reflex, plantar response, certain dynamic gait tasks, and some directed local foot examinations.

Do not assume that lack of documentation proves those examinations were not performed.

Please answer instead:

> **Which of these examinations, or equivalent clinical work, would reasonable neurological practice actually have required in this presentation?**

And:

> **Is the documented examination enough to explain how the competing possibilities were distinguished?**

---

# 5. Do not import later diagnoses backwards

In 2017 the record described the gait in dystonic terms, but the foot pain was not yet established as dystonia.

Later clinicians used dystonia more explicitly, and 2026 genetics linked Parkinsonism and dystonia to Parkin mutations.

Please keep later knowledge out of the 2017 standard-of-care question.

The 2017 question is simply:

> **What should reasonable Neurology have done with painful feet plus a very abnormal neurological lower limb?**

---

# 6. Harm — what injury may have followed?

The relevant harm is not poor wording or a bad record by itself.

Please consider whether any breach probably caused extra:

- foot pain;
- difficulty walking or standing;
- abnormal loading or recurrent structural foot injury;
- loss of mobility or function;
- loss of work / ordinary activity;
- psychological distress caused by prolonged severe pain.

Please separate harm caused by any breach from harm that would have occurred anyway because of the underlying neurological disease, genuine structural foot disease or treatment side effects.

---

# 7. Causation — what difference would better care probably have made?

For each breach you find, please answer:

1. **What should have been done instead?**
2. **What would it probably have found?**
3. **Would that probably have changed the clinical explanation of the foot problem?**
4. **Would that probably have changed treatment or follow-up?**
5. **Would that probably have reduced pain, loading problems, loss of function or distress?**
6. **From what date?**
7. **How much of the later harm would probably have happened anyway?**

Please label each causal conclusion as:

- probable;
- possible only;
- or indeterminate.

---

# 8. Psychological deterioration

The foot pain existed long before neurological treatment.

The expert is not asked to say Neurology caused the original pain.

The narrower question is:

> **Did the way neurological treatment was started, stopped, adjusted or left insufficiently optimised materially affect the severity of the pre-existing foot pain, and did prolonged severe pain then contribute to the later psychological crisis?**

Please identify any causal steps that can or cannot be supported.

---

# 9. Primary care

The GP sent an integrated problem into Neurology and later received a specialist account in which the feet could be separate / neuropathic / “Not clear.”

Please consider:

> **Would a reasonable GP reading that account understand that the foot–neurology relationship had been adequately investigated?**

If the answer is yes, and if the relationship had not actually been adequately investigated, could that have delayed later neurological reconsideration?

---

# 10. The institutional question

Different services held different pieces of the problem.

Please do not assume that “the hospital” automatically knew everything every clinician knew.

The narrower question is:

> **At each important date, who had enough information to ask the relationship question again, and what would reasonable care have required that person or service to do?**

---

# 11. The core expert questions

Please answer these directly:

1. **What examination should have been done in 2017?**
2. **Was the documented work enough to justify treating the painful feet as possibly separate?**
3. **What evidence supported neuropathy?**
4. **What should have happened after the patient said the feet had not been dealt with?**
5. **What should have happened once neurological medication was shown to change the foot problem?**
6. **When, if ever, did later evidence make continued separation unreasonable?**
7. **What better care would probably have changed?**
8. **What later suffering would probably have been avoided?**

---

# 12. Required answer format

For every breach found, please state:

- the date;
- the information available then;
- what reasonable care required;
- what was not done;
- why that fell below standard;
- what should have happened instead;
- what would probably have changed;
- what harm would probably have been reduced;
- what harm would have happened anyway;
- confidence level.

---

# 13. Final instruction

> **Do not let technical language hide the factual sequence. The patient had disabling foot pain. Neurology found a very abnormal neurological gait. Neurological treatment later changed the foot problem. The expert task is to decide what reasonable Neurology should have done with those facts and what difference better care would probably have made.**
